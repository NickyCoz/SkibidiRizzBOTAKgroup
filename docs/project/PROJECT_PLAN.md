# B. Project scope and learning goals
## 2.1 Target Learners:
Our target learners are beginners studying Computer Science, and Artificial Intelligence who have a basic understanding of computers and introductory programming concepts. We assume that learners are familiar with fundamental programming ideas such as variables, conditional statements, loops, and functions but may have limited experience with algorithm analysis, data structures, and neural networks.

The learning materials will introduce these concepts progressively, using clear explanations, practical examples, visual representations, and simple demonstrations. Advanced mathematical knowledge and prior experience with machine learning will not be required.

## 2.2 Learning Goals

After completing our learning materials, learners should be able to:
* Explain algorithms and describe how algorithms provide step-by-step solutions to computational problems.
* Understand basic algorithm analysis, including the purpose of time complexity and space complexity, and interpret introductory Big-O notation.
* Identify and compare fundamental data structures, including arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, hash tables, trees, and graphs, and explain when each may be useful.
* Describe common algorithms, such as searching and sorting, and explain their basic operations and differences in efficiency.
* Explain the relationship between algorithms and data structures, including how the choice of data structure can affect an algorithm's performance.
* Describe connectionist AI, particularly artificial neural networks, and explain how artificial neurons, layers, weights, and activation functions contribute to learning and prediction.
* Distinguish traditional algorithmic problem-solving from connectionist approaches, recognizing that traditional algorithms generally follow explicitly specified procedures, whereas neural networks learn patterns from data during training.
* Apply introductory concepts to simple problems, select an appropriate data structure or algorithm for a given situation, and explain the reasoning behind the choice.
* Interpret simple neural network examples, including how input data passes through a network to produce an output and how training can adjust weights to improve predictions.

## 2.3 Content Outline

Our project will cover three interconnected areas: **algorithms, data structures, and connectionist artificial intelligence.**

### a. Algorithms

* Definition and characteristics of algorithms.
* Representing algorithms using pseudocode and flowcharts.
* Searching algorithms, including linear search and binary search.
* Sorting algorithms, including bubble sort and merge sort, with other sorting methods introduced where appropriate.
* Introduction to algorithm efficiency, including time complexity, space complexity, and Big-O notation.
* Comparing algorithms based on correctness, efficiency, and suitability for different problems.

### b. Data Structures

* Introduction to data organization and the purpose of data structures.
* Arrays and linked lists.
* Stacks and queues.
* Hash tables and basic hashing concepts.
* Trees and their basic terminology.
* Graphs and their representations.
* Introductory comparisons of data structures based on their operations, advantages, limitations, and common applications.
* The relationship between data structures and algorithm efficiency.

### c. Connectionist Artificial Intelligence

* Introduction to connectionism and its role in artificial intelligence.
* Biological inspiration for artificial neural networks.
* Artificial neurons, inputs, weights, biases, and activation functions.
* Basic neural network architecture, including input, hidden, and output layers.
* Forward propagation and the process of generating predictions.
* The basic idea of training a neural network using examples and adjusting weights.
* Introductory explanations of loss functions, gradient descent, and backpropagation.
* Practical applications of neural networks, such as image recognition, text processing, and pattern classification.

### d. Connections Between the Three Areas

* How algorithms operate on and manipulate data structures.
* How the choice of data structure influences computational efficiency.
* How algorithms are used to train and run neural networks.
* Differences between explicitly programmed procedures and models that learn patterns from data.
* Practical examples illustrating how these concepts contribute to solving computational problems.

## 2.4 Scope Boundaries

The project will prioritize conceptual understanding, practical examples, and beginner-friendly explanations rather than exhaustive coverage of every topic.

**Topics requiring detailed explanation:**

* The basic principles and step-by-step operation of searching and sorting algorithms.
* Fundamental data structures and their common operations.
* Introductory time complexity and Big-O notation.
* The relationships between algorithms, data structures, and computational efficiency.
* The structure and basic operation of artificial neural networks.
* How forward propagation and the general training process work.

**Topics receiving a brief introduction:**

* Advanced algorithm design techniques and formal proofs of correctness.
* Complex graph algorithms and specialized data structures.
* Advanced mathematical analysis of algorithm complexity.
* Detailed neural network mathematics, including extensive calculus and matrix derivations.
* Advanced optimization techniques, specialized neural network architectures, and large-scale deep learning systems.

The final level of detail will be adjusted according to the course materials, available time, and the team's understanding of the selected module. The project will emphasize explanations that beginners can understand and apply rather than attempting to cover every advanced topic.

# C. Planned Learning Resources and Mini Lecture

## 3.1 Shared Markdown Content

Our team will develop a shared Markdown knowledge base that serves as the foundation for all project deliverables. The content will be organized into sections covering algorithms, data structures, and connectionist artificial intelligence, with a final section connecting these areas.

Each topic will include:
- A clear definition and explanation of the main concepts.
- Step-by-step examples and pseudocode where appropriate.
- Diagrams, illustrations, or tables to clarify difficult ideas.
- Practical applications and examples from computer science.
- References to reliable sources used during research.
- Practice questions to reinforce understanding.

Team members will draft their assigned sections in Markdown and submit them for peer review. The team will use GitHub to organize files, track changes, and document individual contributions. Before producing the final learning resources, the team will review the shared content for accuracy, clarity, consistency, and appropriate difficulty.

## 3.2 Study Guide and Test Bank

The study guide will provide a structured introduction to the three subject areas. It will be organized into chapters, beginning with foundational concepts and progressing toward more complex ideas.

The proposed structure is:

1. Introduction to algorithms and algorithmic thinking.
2. Searching, sorting, and introductory algorithm analysis.
3. Fundamental data structures and their operations.
4. Relationships between algorithms, data structures, and efficiency.
5. Introduction to connectionist AI and artificial neural networks.
6. Neural network structure, forward propagation, and basic training concepts.
7. Applications and connections between the three subject areas.

Each chapter will contain learning objectives, key terminology, explanations, worked examples, diagrams where useful, and a short summary.

The accompanying test bank will include multiple-choice questions, short-answer questions, concept-comparison questions, and practical problem-solving exercises. Questions will test both conceptual understanding and the ability to apply knowledge. Answer keys and brief explanations will help learners identify mistakes and review difficult concepts.

## 3.3 PPT Slides

The PowerPoint presentation will summarize the study guide's most important concepts in a visual and accessible format. Slides will prioritize clarity rather than reproducing large amounts of textbook-style text.

Planned visual elements include:
- Flowcharts and pseudocode illustrating algorithm steps.
- Worked examples comparing searching and sorting algorithms.
- Diagrams showing the structures of arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, and graphs.
- Comparison tables explaining the advantages and limitations of different data structures.
- Neural network diagrams showing input, hidden, and output layers.
- Illustrations of how information passes through a neural network during prediction.
- Examples demonstrating how algorithms and data structures support AI applications.

The slides will follow the same terminology, examples, and conceptual sequence as the shared Markdown content and study guide. Team members will review the slides for readability, accuracy, and consistency before final delivery.

## 3.4 Video Tutorials

The video tutorials will provide narrated explanations and demonstrations of concepts that are easier to understand through step-by-step visualization.

The proposed tutorial topics are:

1. **Searching and sorting algorithms:** Demonstrating how an algorithm processes data and how different approaches compare.
2. **Fundamental data structures:** Visualizing operations such as adding, removing, searching for, and accessing elements.
3. **Algorithm efficiency:** Introducing time complexity and Big-O notation using simple examples.
4. **Artificial neural networks:** Explaining neurons, layers, weights, inputs, and outputs through diagrams and animations.
5. **Neural network learning:** Demonstrating the basic idea of prediction, error measurement, and weight adjustment during training.

Each tutorial will have a defined learning objective, a short introduction, a structured explanation, and a concluding recap. Where appropriate, the team will include pseudocode, simple programming demonstrations, or visual simulations.

The videos will complement the study guide and slides rather than repeat them word for word. The study guide will provide detailed reference material, the slides will support visual learning and presentations, and the videos will demonstrate processes that benefit from narration or animation.

## 3.5 Live Mini Lecture
Our team proposes a mini lecture on **how a simple searching algorithm works and how its efficiency depends on the problem and data organization**.

The lecture will introduce linear search and binary search using a small collection of numbers. We will demonstrate how linear search checks elements sequentially and how binary search repeatedly narrows the search interval when the data is sorted.

Learners will participate in a short activity in which they identify a target value using each method and compare the number of comparisons required. This will help illustrate why algorithm choice matters and why binary search requires sorted data.

The mini lecture is suitable for beginners because it uses a familiar problem, requires minimal mathematical background, and provides an opportunity for audience participation. It also connects algorithm design, data organization, and efficiency.

The lecture will be supported by a small set of slides, a worked example, and a brief practice question. The team will rehearse the explanation and check the timing before the scheduled presentation.

The final mini-lecture topic will be confirmed against the teacher's approved topic menu.

# D. Workflow, Schedule, and Coordination

## 4.1 Project Workflow

The team will use a staged workflow to ensure that the learning content is accurate and consistent before being converted into the final deliverables.

**Step 1: Research and understand the content**

Members will study the assigned course materials and consult reliable supplementary sources when necessary. Each member will record key concepts, definitions, examples, and references for their assigned topics. Members will also discuss unfamiliar concepts with the team to develop a shared understanding.

**Step 2: Develop the Markdown foundation**

The team will organize the content into a consistent Markdown structure. Each section will contain explanations, examples, and references. Team members will review one another's contributions for correctness, clarity, and suitability for beginners.

**Step 3: Review and approve the content**

Before full production, the team will check the Markdown content for factual errors, unclear explanations, missing examples, and inconsistent terminology. Corrections will be made to the shared source before they are incorporated into the study guide, slides, or videos.

**Step 4: Produce the learning resources**

Using the approved Markdown content, team members will develop the study guide, test bank, PPT slides, and video tutorials. Each deliverable will have its own production tasks and internal deadlines.

**Step 5: Test and rehearse**

The team will test practice questions, review visual explanations, check video clarity, and rehearse the live mini lecture. Where possible, team members will ask classmates or other learners to identify confusing explanations and provide feedback.

**Step 6: Revise and finalize**

The team will incorporate feedback, correct errors, and check that all deliverables remain consistent with the shared content. Final files, references, contribution records, and required documentation will be reviewed before submission.

## 4.5 Risks and Contingency Plans

**Unfamiliar tools:** Some members may have limited experience with Markdown or GitHub. The team will share basic instructions, practise essential operations, and help members resolve problems early.

**Uneven workloads:** Tasks will be assigned according to members' strengths and development needs, while ensuring that everyone contributes substantively. The coordinator will review progress and redistribute work if necessary.

**Delayed tasks:** Members will communicate delays as early as possible. The team will prioritize essential content, identify dependent tasks, and provide assistance or reassign work when needed.

**Inconsistent deliverables:** All resources will use the shared Markdown content as their common foundation. Significant changes will be recorded, and affected materials will be checked after updates.

**Technical or production problems:** The team will keep working copies and backups of important files, test video and presentation tools in advance, and prepare alternative demonstrations if technical issues arise.

**Insufficient review time:** Internal deadlines will be set before official deadlines, and the team will reserve time for peer review
