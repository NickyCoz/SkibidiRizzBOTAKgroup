## 1. General Instructions

Revise and finalize your Study Guide and Test Bank so that beginners can **explore, understand, practise, and enjoy learning your module independently**.

Build on V1.0 using relevant teacher feedback, peer review, mini-lecture experience, and your own testing. Correct errors, complete missing content, improve weak activities, and strengthen the learning sequence.

**The Study Guide should be an engaging learning experience, not simply a textbook for passive reading.** Help learners solve problems, experiment, make predictions, and understand the results.

Ensure that the final guide, slides, and videos work together as a coherent learning system. Their concepts and answers should agree, while each resource makes good use of its medium.

Read the **Shared Assignment Rules**, the **Study Guide and Test Bank Assessment Rubric**, and any relevant feedback files. The quality checklist is included below.

## 2. Specific Requirements

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **November 28, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**

### A. Submit a Complete Final Study Guide

Finalize all core topics within your agreed module scope.

Include:

- Title, team, module, final version information, and a usable table of contents.
    
- Learning goals, prerequisites, and suggestions for using the materials.
    
- Accurate, beginner-friendly explanations in a logical sequence.
    
- Concrete examples, demonstrations, and purposeful visuals.
    
- Hands-on activities and application questions.
    
- Hints, explained answers, expected outcomes, or other useful feedback.
    
- Summaries or key takeaways and a glossary.
    
- References and introduced learning resources.
    

Remove placeholders, unfinished instructions, obsolete draft notes, and confusing duplicate material.

Review the whole guide for consistency. Sections written by different members should form a connected experience with a shared voice and recognizable identity.

**Submit the complete final guide, not only the sections changed since V1.0.**

### B. Improve the Learning Experience

Check each major topic from a beginner’s perspective:

- Is the purpose clear?
    
- Are necessary prerequisites explained?
    
- Does a concrete example make the idea understandable?
    
- Can learners do something meaningful with the idea?
    
- Can they check their understanding and learn from mistakes?
    
- Is the next step clear?
    

Strengthen activities that previously involved only reading, watching, or clicking without reasoning. Ask learners to predict, compare, explain, modify, test, or solve.

Where relevant, improve activities using platforms such as Machine Learning for Kids, TensorFlow Playground, ELIZA or A.L.I.C.E. simulations, Little Man Computer, or Prolog.

**The activity design matters more than the platform.** Include clear instructions, questions, and feedback that connect the learner’s actions to the intended concept.

Keep useful humor, stories, drama, memes, or recurring characters. Refine elements that distract, require too much explanation, or feel disconnected from the rest of the guide.

Use consistent headings, activity labels, tone, and visual design across the learning resources. Place related explanations and visuals close together.

### C. Finalize and Verify the Test Bank

Submit a separate, editable Word Test Bank containing at least:

|Question type|Minimum|Required answer support|
|---|---|---|
|**True/False**|20|Correct answer, brief explanation, and correction of false statements.|
|**Multiple Choice**|20|Correct choice or choices and an explanation of the reasoning.|
|**Open-ended**|6|A sample answer or expected key points, recognizing valid alternatives where appropriate.|

Open-ended questions should require developed reasoning, application, comparison, analysis, or evaluation. They should normally invite more than 100 words or equivalent reasoning through diagrams, calculations, or worked steps.

Check that:

- Questions cover the important topics in a balanced way.
    
- Wording and instructions are clear.
    
- Multiple-choice alternatives are plausible.
    
- Questions are not repeated merely to increase the count.
    
- All necessary information is included.
    
- Question numbers and references to guide sections are accurate.
    
- Answers agree with the final guide, Markdown, slides, and videos.
    

Place the questions first and the answer key in a clearly labelled later section.

Have someone other than the author check every final question and answer. Earlier checks may be retained for unchanged questions; recheck every revised or added question and any affected by content corrections.

**More questions do not automatically improve the Test Bank.** Replace weak questions and correct unreliable answers before expanding it.

### D. Respond to Feedback and Show Improvement

Provide a concise **Improvement Summary** covering the guide, Test Bank, and any supplements.

|Feedback or problem|What changed and why|Where to find it and how it was checked|
|---|---|---|
|A specific issue identified through feedback or testing|The correction or improvement made|Section, question number, or supplement; verification result|

Use relevant teacher feedback, general feedback documents, peer review, mini-lecture responses, and your own review.

For the most important improvements, include a short before-and-after excerpt, screenshot, or test result **beside or below the explanation**.

Focus on meaningful changes, such as:

- Correcting a misconception.
    
- Replacing an unclear example.
    
- Making an activity workable for beginners.
    
- Improving a question and its answer.
    
- Repairing navigation or access.
    
- Reorganizing a section to improve the learning sequence.
    

If you do not adopt a relevant suggestion, explain why. Do not invent feedback or make unnecessary changes merely to increase the number of revisions.

**The quality of the Improvement Summary will be considered alongside the final materials.** It should make the development understandable without requiring the teacher to reconstruct it from earlier submissions.

### E. Finalize Resources and Optional Supplements

Check every recommended video, tutorial, platform, and other online resource.

Retain a brief introduction explaining:

- What the resource is and who provides it.
    
- Why it is relevant.
    
- Which section or video segment to use.
    
- What learners should do or notice.
    
- Any access requirements or important limitations.
    

Remove broken, unsuitable, or redundant resources. Do not leave a long list of unexplained links.

If your team includes optional quizzes, notebooks, applications, games, or a learning website:

- Complete the features needed for the stated learning purpose.
    
- Provide clear setup and learner instructions.
    
- Include required source files, data, and supporting assets.
    
- Test the experience from a learner’s perspective.
    
- Check permissions, quiz feedback, copying or running settings, and any account requirements.
    
- Identify your team’s contribution and acknowledge borrowed components.
    

Working links remain permitted for external resources and online supplements under this assignment’s exception to the shared submission rules. However, the required guide and Test Bank must still be submitted as complete files.

Include representative screenshots and appropriate fallback material so that the learning purpose remains understandable if an external service is unavailable. Screenshots do not replace demonstrating that a team-created supplement works.

**Optional supplements remain eligible for extra credit under the published rubric.** They do not replace required content, and teams without them remain eligible for full marks on the required work.

### F. Test the Final Learning System

Ask a teammate or peer who did not write a selected section to use it as a learner.

Have them follow the explanation, attempt an activity, and use the feedback to check their result. Record where they became confused and what you improved.

Also check that:

- Definitions, examples, and answers match the final Markdown content.
    
- References to slides, videos, questions, and files are correct.
    
- Learners can move between resources without losing the learning sequence.
    
- Images, tables, code, and formulas display properly.
    
- The submitted files include everything needed for the required activities.
    

When correcting an error, update every affected resource.

Record significant testing, revisions, and AI assistance in the project and personal logs, with evidence beside or below the relevant entry. The Improvement Summary may include selected results without duplicating the full logs.
