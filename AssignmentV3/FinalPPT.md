Revise and finalize your slides into a clear, engaging teaching resource for a large classroom of beginners.

## 2. Specific Requirements

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **November 28, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**

### A. Complete the Teaching Story

Finalize the slides for your agreed module scope, including:

- An engaging opening question, problem, or scenario.
    
- Clear learning goals and a brief roadmap.
    
- A logical sequence of essential concepts.
    
- Concrete examples and visual explanations.
    
- Purposeful demonstrations and audience activities.
    
- A conclusion that connects the main ideas and provides a memorable takeaway.
    
- Specific next steps for practice or further learning.
    

Remove placeholders, unfinished explanations, unnecessary repetition, and material that distracts from the central teaching purpose.

Check transitions between sections, especially where different members prepared different parts. The presentation should feel like one deliberately designed lesson or connected series of lessons.

**Submit the complete module slide collection, not only the slides used for your mini lecture or the slides revised since V1.0.**

There is no fixed slide count. Completeness depends on the teaching story, not the number of slides.

### B. Improve Explanations and Classroom Readability

Check every slide in slideshow mode.

- Give each slide one main teaching purpose.
    
- Keep audience-facing text concise.
    
- Move supporting detail into presenter notes or the Study Guide.
    
- Use readable text, strong contrast, and clear visual hierarchy.
    
- Enlarge diagram labels, code, tables, and relevant screenshot details.
    
- Place related labels and explanations close to the relevant visual.
    
- Divide complex processes into manageable steps.
    
- Use animation only when it helps the audience follow the explanation.
    

**Do not solve overcrowding by shrinking the text.** Simplify the slide, split it, or move detail elsewhere.

Aim for approximately **28–32 pt or larger for main text**, then judge the actual projected result. Where possible, inspect the slides from the back of the classroom.

Recheck the meaning of each diagram and example. A polished visual must still represent the concept accurately.

### C. Refine Audience Activities and Demonstrations

Ensure that participation helps learners use the ideas rather than merely stay busy.

For each activity, provide:

- Clear instructions.
    
- Enough information to attempt the task.
    
- Realistic thinking or discussion time.
    
- An answer, explanation, or debrief.
    
- A connection to the intended learning outcome.
    

Suitable activities include predicting an output, identifying an error, comparing strategies, tracing a process, or discussing a short question with a neighbor.

Use mini-lecture experience where relevant. If classmates misunderstood a question, could not read a diagram, or struggled with an example, revise the corresponding material.

For demonstrations:

- Prepare the starting state, settings, and supporting files.
    
- Explain what the audience should observe.
    
- Show only the steps needed for the learning goal.
    
- Interpret the result.
    
- Include an offline fallback for demonstrations that depend on an online service.
    

Make activities practical for a large class. Avoid designs that require lengthy responses from many individual students.

### D. Strengthen Engagement and a Consistent Identity

Apply the three presentation rules:

**1. Cut to the chase.**  
Reach meaningful content promptly. Keep introductions and roadmaps brief.

**2. Show, not tell.**  
Use examples, diagrams, comparisons, and demonstrations to reveal how ideas work.

**3. Save the best for last.**  
Build toward a strong application, demonstration, or insight that brings the teaching story together.

Refine humor, storytelling, drama, recurring characters, or surprising examples where they help the audience understand and remember.

Maintain a consistent tone, visual style, terminology, and approach to activities across the slides, guide, and videos. Remove distracting effects and elements that feel disconnected from the learning experience.

### E. Finalize Presenter Notes and Resource Connections

Provide sufficient presenter notes for another prepared team member to teach the material.

Include, where relevant:

- Explanations and reasoning beyond the visible slide text.
    
- Key points and misconceptions to address.
    
- Transitions between ideas.
    
- Demonstration steps and setup requirements.
    
- Audience questions, expected answers, and approximate activity timings.
    
- Sources and media credits.
    
- References to related Study Guide sections, Test Bank questions, and Video Tutorials.
    

A word-for-word script is not required. Notes should be useful and easy to follow rather than simply repeat the slides.

Update all references to match the final materials. For example, use the correct section heading, question number, or video filename.

**Supporting detail may differ across resources, but definitions, examples, and answers must agree.** Carry corrections into the final Markdown and every affected learning resource.

### F. Explain Substantive Improvements

Provide a concise **Improvement Summary**.

|Feedback or problem|What changed and why|Location and verification|
|---|---|---|
|A specific issue identified through feedback, review, or rehearsal|The correction or improvement made|Deck name and slide number; how the result was checked|

For the most important changes, place readable before-and-after excerpts or screenshots **beside or below the explanation**.

Focus on improvements such as:

- Repairing a weak teaching sequence.
    
- Correcting an inaccurate explanation or diagram.
    
- Replacing dense text with an effective visual.
    
- Improving an audience question and its debrief.
    
- Making a demonstration reliable.
    
- Adding missing presenter guidance.
    
- Strengthening connections with the guide and videos.
    

If a relevant suggestion was not adopted, explain why. Do not manufacture changes merely to increase the revision count.

**The quality of the Improvement Summary will be considered alongside the final slides.** It should make the development understandable without requiring the teacher to reconstruct it from earlier submissions.

### G. Rehearse and Verify the Final Version

Have another member review the final deck and use the presenter notes to rehearse representative sections, particularly those substantially revised.

Check:

- Whether the explanation flows logically.
    
- Whether examples, diagrams, and answers are accurate.
    
- Whether audience instructions and timings are workable.
    
- Whether visuals remain readable during presentation.
    
- Whether animations, audio, video, and demonstrations work.
    
- Whether the conclusion resolves the lesson’s central question.
    

Test the submitted copy, including supporting files, from a different folder or computer where possible.

Record significant rehearsal findings, corrections, and AI assistance in the project and personal logs, with evidence **beside or below the relevant entry**.
