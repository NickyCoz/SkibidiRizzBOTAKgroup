## 2. Specific Requirements

**Deadline: November 28, 2026, at 23:59 Beijing time.**

### A. Record Your Learning and Work Throughout Stage 3

Write entries as meaningful learning or work occurs. Arrange them chronologically and include:

- **Date and, where useful, time.**
- **What you learned or worked on.**
- **Your specific actions and contribution.**
- **The outcome, supported by relevant evidence.**
- **Problems, questions, or misconceptions encountered.**
- **What you learned and what you need to do next.**

Relevant activities may include:

- Learning concepts through classes, mini-lectures, readings, exercises, or experiments.
- Revising Markdown content and checking sources.
- Improving the Study Guide, Test Bank, slides, or videos.
- Testing activities, checking answers, or reviewing teammates’ work.
- Preparing and delivering the mini-lecture or final showcase.
- Resolving inconsistencies across deliverables.
- Learning from another team’s materials.
- Identifying gaps and preparing for the final examination.

Describe what actually happened. “Worked on the Study Guide” is too vague; explain which section you changed, why, and how you checked it.

There is no minimum daily word count. Record meaningful activity without inventing entries for inactive days. Clearly label any retrospective additions.

### B. Show Learning as Well as Task Completion

Your log should make your thinking visible.

For example, explain:

- What you initially misunderstood.
- What example, experiment, explanation, or feedback helped.
- How you tested your new understanding.
- What you can now explain or do independently.
- What remains uncertain.

Learning includes unsuccessful attempts when you explain what they revealed and how you responded.

A useful entry might describe how you discovered an incorrect test-bank answer, checked the concept using a reliable source and a small example, corrected the explanation, and checked related slides for the same error.

**Your learning should extend beyond your production role and your team’s topic.** Include relevant experiences from the wider course. A member responsible for video editing should still demonstrate learning about CS and AI.

### C. Place Evidence Beside or Below Each Entry

Place evidence **beside or immediately below the relevant entry**, so that the explanation and evidence reinforce each other.

This follows an important media-design principle: related information should be placed close together so that readers can readily understand the connection.

Suitable evidence includes:

- Annotated notes, diagrams, or worked exercises.
- Code and its output, with an explanation.
- Screenshots or excerpts of your contributions.
- Before-and-after revisions.
- Relevant GitHub changes or review comments.
- Test results, feedback, and corrections.
- Selected AI interactions and your verification work.

Add a short caption explaining what the evidence demonstrates and identifying your contribution where work was shared.

**Do not place evidence in a separate section or appendix.** External links and repository references may supplement the record, but the submitted document should contain enough evidence to understand and assess your work.

Select useful evidence rather than filling pages with screenshots. Photographs of working, attendance screenshots, or long chat histories usually reveal less than the actual outcomes.

### D. Document Significant AI Use and Your Own Judgment

For significant AI-assisted learning or project work, record:

- The tool, date, and purpose.
- Relevant prompts, outputs, and follow-up exchanges.
- How you checked the response.
- What you accepted, revised, or rejected, and why.
- What you personally learned or contributed.

Keep this evidence beside the related entry. You do not need to reproduce every conversation, but a statement such as “used AI to improve the content” is insufficient.

Distinguish between receiving an explanation and understanding it. Where appropriate, demonstrate understanding through your own explanation, an independent solution, a test, or a corrected example.

AI may help organize or polish your writing under the Shared Assignment Rules. **It must not invent experiences, evidence, contributions, or reflections for you.** Preserve your own voice and judgment.

### E. Write a Final Reflection on the Whole Course

After your Stage 3 entries, add a clearly labeled **Final Reflection**.

Use specific experiences from across the course to address the following areas. You may organize them into connected paragraphs or short subsections.

**1. Growth in CS and AI understanding**

What important ideas do you understand better now? Explain significant changes in your thinking, including misconceptions you corrected.

Consider both your team’s topic and other course topics. Support your claims with examples of what you can now explain, compare, apply, or demonstrate.

**2. Growth as an independent learner**

Which learning approaches helped you most? Consider practice, experiments, reading, discussion, teaching others, and checking your own understanding.

Explain what you learned about using AI productively while retaining responsibility for your thinking.

**3. Your contribution and collaboration**

What were your most meaningful contributions across the three stages? Explain their value and distinguish your work from shared work.

Reflect on reliability, communication, review, and problem solving. Discuss a specific collaboration challenge and what you learned from it.

**4. Designing materials that teach**

What did you learn about helping beginners understand difficult ideas?

Consider how the Study Guide, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials require different design choices while forming a coherent learning experience. Use an example of a design decision or revision you helped make.

**5. Limitations and next steps**

What do you still find difficult? What would you change if you started the project again?

End with a short, practical learning plan. Identify what you will study or practise next and how you will check your progress.

Avoid general statements such as “I learned a lot” or “teamwork is important” without explanation. **A thoughtful reflection connects experience, interpretation, and future action.**

You may reuse a relevant example from your brief reflection in the team’s Final Project Report, but develop your personal learning here. There is no need to repeat the team’s full project narrative.

### F. Keep the Writing Personal, Honest, and Readable

Write in your own voice. Small stories, diagrams, humor, and honest descriptions of difficulties are welcome when they help communicate your experience.

Chinese or bilingual writing is acceptable for clarity, consistent with the earlier personal-log assignments. Use technical terms accurately.

Do not copy another member’s entries or replace individual experiences with a shared team narrative. Members may describe the same event, but each should explain their own role and learning.

Quality is judged through **specificity, evidence, reflection, and demonstrated learning**, rather than length, polished language, or claims of effort.

### G. Combine All Members’ Work into One Document

Submit **ONE Word or PDF document** containing every member’s Stage 3 log and final reflection.

Include:

- A cover page identifying the team, assignment, and reporting period.
- A contents list identifying all members.
- A separate section for each member, starting on a new page.
- Each member’s name as it appears in the class roster, student ID, team name, and reporting period.
- That member’s dated entries, followed by their final reflection.

Use consistent headings and readable formatting while preserving individual voices. Each member must check their own section after the document is combined.

Use a clear filename, such as:

`TeamName_Stage3_PersonalLogs_FinalReflections.pdf`

**Do not ZIP this single document.**

Submit it through the designated assignment submission area. **Do not post personal logs in the public repository or class resource-sharing space.** Remove credentials and unrelated private information from evidence.
