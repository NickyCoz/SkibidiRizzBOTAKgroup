## 1. General Instructions

Revise and finalize your tutorial collection so that beginners can learn effectively through **clear explanations, purposeful demonstrations, and engaging audiovisual storytelling**.

Use relevant teacher feedback, peer review, mini-lecture experience, and viewer testing to improve the content, pacing, visuals, narration, and learning activities.

**Quality matters more than quantity.** Your goal is to produce videos that learners want to watch and can learn from. More videos, longer runtime, or more recording hours do not automatically demonstrate better work.

The final videos should reinforce the Study Guide and Test Bank and PPT Slides as parts of one coherent learning system. Maintain consistent concepts and a recognizable identity while using video to show processes, develop explanations, and make learning memorable.

Read the **Shared Assignment Rules**, the **Video Tutorials Assessment Rubric**, and relevant feedback files. The quality checklist is included below.

## 2. Specific Requirements

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **November 28, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**  
**Number of videos:** **1–8 videos in total**  
**Format and size:** **MP4; no larger than 250 MB per video, with no exceptions**

### A. Finalize a Coherent Tutorial Collection

Review the collection as a whole and confirm that it provides a connected route through your module’s essential ideas.

Each video should have:

- A descriptive title and clear learning purpose.
    
- Necessary prerequisites introduced or identified.
    
- A logical explanation supported by concrete examples.
    
- A demonstration or application where appropriate.
    
- An opportunity for viewers to check their understanding.
    
- A meaningful conclusion and relevant next step.
    

Combine trivial or overlapping videos, remove unnecessary repetition, and fill important conceptual gaps.

**Eight videos is the maximum, not a target.** Fewer well-designed videos can communicate the material more effectively.

Submit the **complete final collection**, including unchanged videos that remain part of the learning sequence. Do not submit only new recordings or revised segments.

### B. Improve Teaching and Engagement

Review whether the narration helps viewers understand the reasoning behind what they see.

**Videos must do more than read the slides aloud.** Explain mechanisms, work through examples, interpret results, and address likely misunderstandings.

Apply the three presentation rules:

**1. Cut to the chase.**  
Reach meaningful content promptly. Shorten unnecessary introductions, branding sequences, and background information.

**2. Show, not tell.**  
Use demonstrations, diagrams, comparisons, animations, or physical examples to make ideas visible.

**3. Save the best for last.**  
Build toward a useful application, revealing result, or insight that brings the explanation together.

Refine humor, stories, drama, recurring characters, or other creative elements where they support learning. Remove elements that distract or consume time without helping viewers understand.

Keep the collection’s tone, visual identity, terminology, and recurring examples consistent with the guide and slides.

### C. Edit for Purposeful Pacing

Watch the videos critically and remove:

- Repeated explanations that add little.
    
- False starts and avoidable mistakes.
    
- Long periods of setup, loading, or menu navigation.
    
- Unnecessary pauses, filler, and decorative sequences.
    
- Examples that do not contribute to the learning goal.
    

Preserve enough time for learners to inspect visuals and follow difficult reasoning. Do not make an overlong explanation shorter simply by accelerating the narration.

There is no minimum runtime. Longer videos are acceptable when their content and pacing justify the length and they meet the file-size limit. Update chapter timestamps for longer videos.

**Revision does not always require recording everything again.** Retain effective sections and concentrate effort on corrections and improvements that matter. However, significant teaching errors must be corrected in the video itself, not merely mentioned in a separate document.

### D. Strengthen Demonstrations and Learning Checks

Check that demonstrations explain both **what happens and why**.

Make relevant starting conditions, settings, inputs, and outputs clear. If editing skips a lengthy process, explain what was omitted.

Include opportunities for viewers to pause and:

- Predict a result.
    
- Trace a process.
    
- Identify an error.
    
- Compare alternatives.
    
- Modify an example.
    
- Explain a decision.
    

Provide enough information to attempt the task, followed by an explained answer or expected outcome.

Use feedback from the mini lecture or learner testing to improve confusing questions and examples. Update references to the final Study Guide sections, Test Bank questions, and supporting activity files.

### E. Finalize Audio, Visuals, and Compression

Check the final exported videos for:

- Intelligible speech and reasonably consistent volume.
    
- Controlled background noise.
    
- Narration that matches the relevant visuals.
    
- Readable text, code, diagrams, and captions.
    
- Appropriate highlighting, framing, and zooming.
    
- Accurate pronunciation of technical terms.
    
- Corrected captions where included or needed.
    
- Music and effects that support rather than compete with the explanation.
    

**Every video must be properly compressed to no more than 250 MB. There are no exceptions.**

Choose export settings that preserve the details required for learning without creating unnecessarily large files. Check the actual file size and leave a margin below the limit.

If a video is too large, improve its editing or export settings, or reorganize the lesson within the eight-video maximum. Do not split it into arbitrary fragments or use a ZIP archive to bypass the requirement.

**Judge quality from the final compressed file.** A clear original recording does not compensate for blurry text, distorted sound, or unreadable demonstrations after export.

### F. Explain Substantive Improvements

Provide a concise **Improvement Summary** in the tutorial guide.

|Feedback or problem|What changed and why|Location and verification|
|---|---|---|
|A specific issue identified through feedback or testing|The correction, edit, replacement, or reorganization made|Final video filename and timestamp; how the result was checked|

For important changes, include readable screenshots or short narration excerpts **beside or below the explanation**. Where useful, identify both the previous and final timestamps.

Meaningful improvements might include:

- Correcting an inaccurate explanation.
    
- Replacing slide-reading with a worked demonstration.
    
- Cutting repetition while preserving the reasoning.
    
- Improving a learning check and its feedback.
    
- Enlarging code or diagram labels.
    
- Repairing audio or captions.
    
- Reducing file size while retaining readability.
    
- Improving the connection to a guide activity.
    

Explain relevant suggestions that were not adopted. Do not invent feedback or make unnecessary edits merely to increase the number of changes.

**The quality of the Improvement Summary will be considered alongside the final videos.** It should make the development understandable without requiring the teacher to reconstruct it from earlier submissions.

### G. Verify the Final Learning Experience

Have another team member watch **every final compressed video from beginning to end**.

Ask someone who did not create a selected tutorial to follow it as a beginner and attempt its learning check. Identify where they become confused and use the results to improve the tutorial.

Confirm that:

- Explanations, examples, and answers agree with the final Markdown, guide, and slides.
    
- Supporting files match the demonstrated version.
    
- Video titles, filenames, viewing order, and timestamps are accurate.
    
- References to other resources lead to the intended material.
    
- Corrections have reached all affected deliverables.
    

Record significant testing, revisions, and AI assistance in the project and personal logs, with evidence **beside or below the relevant entry**.
