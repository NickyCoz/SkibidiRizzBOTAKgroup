## 2. Specific Requirements

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team

### A. Complete and Check the Markdown Content

Finalize all core topics within your agreed module scope.

Include:

- A module overview, intended learners, prerequisites, and learning outcomes.
    
- A logical sequence of topics and subtopics.
    
- Accurate, beginner-friendly explanations.
    
- Worked examples, demonstrations, and purposeful visuals.
    
- Meaningful learner activities or application questions.
    
- Checked answers, expected outcomes, or explanatory feedback.
    
- References and attribution for borrowed material.
    

**Required core sections must contain finished learning content.** Empty headings, placeholder text, unchecked AI output, and promises of future development do not count as complete work.

Remove obsolete draft warnings and abandoned material from the main learning path. Clearly distinguish any optional future extensions from the completed scope.

Review the module as a whole. Check for repeated explanations, inconsistent terminology, missing prerequisites, and abrupt transitions that may have resulted from different members writing different sections.

### B. Incorporate Feedback and Explain Important Revisions

Use relevant feedback from:

- Stage 2 assessment and general feedback documents.
    
- Peer review, where available.
    
- Your mini lecture, including audience responses and questions.
    
- Team content review and learner testing.
    

Include a concise **Final Revision Summary** in the submission report:

|Feedback or problem|Revision and reason|Location and verification|
|---|---|---|
|What needed improvement and how it was identified|What changed and why|Markdown file and heading; how the team checked the result|

Focus on substantive improvements, such as correcting a misconception, completing a missing topic, improving an activity, or resolving a contradiction across resources.

For the most important revisions, place a short before-and-after excerpt or other supporting evidence **beside or below the corresponding explanation**.

If a relevant suggestion was not adopted, explain the reason. Do not invent feedback or manufacture unnecessary changes merely to make the revision summary longer.

Submit the **complete final content**, not only the revised sections.

### C. Check Consistency Across the Learning System

Compare the final Markdown content with the Study Guide and Test Bank, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials.

Check that:

- Definitions, terminology, notation, and factual claims agree.
    
- Shared examples and answers are consistent.
    
- Activities have usable instructions and appropriate feedback.
    
- References to sections, questions, videos, and supporting files are accurate.
    
- Corrections made in one resource have reached every affected resource.
    

The resources may differ in detail and presentation. Slides should support classroom teaching, videos should explain and demonstrate, and the guide should support independent learning.

**Consistency does not mean copying identical content into every format.** The underlying ideas must agree while each resource serves its purpose.

Update the resource mapping developed in Stage 2. Identify where each major Markdown topic is used in the guide, test bank, slides, or videos. Mark topics not selected for a particular medium clearly.

### D. Organize the Final Repository

Continue using the same repository. Make the final project easy to understand and navigate.

Update the README to include:

- Team name, selected module, members, and GitHub usernames.
    
- Project purpose, intended learners, and learning goals.
    
- A clear starting point and recommended reading order.
    
- A brief explanation of the folder structure.
    
- Locations of the Markdown content, assets, and learning resources.
    
- Instructions for opening or running supporting materials.
    
- Final version information and any known limitations.
    

Use meaningful filenames and folders. Keep supporting assets with the relevant content and use relative paths for local images and files where appropriate.

Remove temporary exports, confusing duplicate “final” files, and unused material from the main project structure. Older development should remain traceable through Git history.

**Do not delete and recreate the repository merely to make it appear tidy.**

Large videos may remain outside GitHub, with their locations identified in the README. Their separate assignment submissions must still contain the actual required video files.

Keep personal logs, private information, and credentials out of publicly accessible repository content.
