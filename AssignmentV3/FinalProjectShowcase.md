## Final Project Showcase (20XP)

**Opened:** 星期一, 12 十月 2026, 12:14 AM

**Due:** 星期六, 28 十一月 2026, 11:54 PM

## 1. General Instructions

Your team has created [learning materials](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/folder/view.php?id=542344 "Learning materials") throughout the semester. Now help your classmates discover **what you made, why it is useful, and how to start using it**.

Prepare a short showcase delivered either **live in class** or through **a prerecorded video played during class**. Both formats have the same purpose and requirements.

Your audience should leave knowing:

- What topics your materials help them learn.
    
- What makes your materials useful and engaging.
    
- How your Study Guide and Test Bank, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials work together.
    
- Where to find the materials and what to try first.
    

**Show the learning experience you have created.** Select concrete examples that make classmates want to use your materials for learning, practice, and exam preparation. A list of filenames or a speech about how hard your team worked will not achieve this purpose.

Read these instructions together with the **Shared Assignment Rules**. The quality checklist is integrated below.

## 2. Specific Requirements

### A. Format and Time Limit

Choose either:

- **Live showcase:** Present and demonstrate your materials in class. You may include short prerecorded clips.
    
- **Prerecorded showcase:** Submit one complete video to be played during your team’s slot. Screen recording with clear narration is acceptable; appearing on camera is optional.
    

Each team has **a maximum five-minute slot, including changeover**:

- **Showcase content: maximum 4 minutes.**
    
- **Changeover: up to 30 seconds.**
    

For a prerecorded showcase, any spoken introduction or closing remarks must fit within the same content limit. Shorter showcases are welcome if they achieve the purpose.

Prepare carefully so that all teams can finish within the available class time. There is no separate question-and-answer period within the schedule; classmates can follow up afterward.

**Submission deadline:** Follow the deadline shown in Lexue. Your presentation files must be submitted and ready **before the showcase class begins**.

### B. Show What Classmates Can Learn

Briefly introduce your team, topic, and intended learners. Explain the learning value in concrete terms.

For example:

> “If you can name the three AI paradigms but still struggle to explain their differences, our materials let you compare them through three small experiments.”

Show the actual final materials, including:

- **Study Guide and Test Bank:** How learners explore concepts, complete activities, and check their understanding.
    
- **PPT Slides:** How the slides explain the main ideas through a clear teaching story and useful visuals.
    
- **Video Tutorials:** How the videos explain difficult ideas, demonstrate processes, or guide learners through examples.
    

You may also show a well-designed optional supplement, such as a quiz, notebook, simulation, game, or learning website.

Give the audience a clear view of the whole collection, then spend most of your time on its strongest features. You do not need to open every file or demonstrate every activity.

### C. Demonstrate a Useful Learning Experience

Include **at least one concrete demonstration** of how someone would learn with your materials.

For example, you could:

- Show a short activity, invite a prediction, and reveal the explanation.
    
- Demonstrate a game or simulation and explain the concept it teaches.
    
- Show how a difficult idea becomes clearer through a diagram and a short video excerpt.
    
- Walk through a test-bank question and show how the answer explanation helps learners correct a misconception.
    

Explain how the three main deliverables reinforce one another. A simple learning route might be:

> Start with the PPT overview → watch a video demonstration → complete the Study Guide activity → check understanding with the Test Bank.

Adapt the route to your materials. **Make it clear what learners should do, rather than simply showing that the files exist.**

Demonstrations must fit the time limit. Prepare examples in advance and avoid lengthy loading, installation, typing, or file searching.

### D. Make the Materials Easy to Find and Use

Show classmates exactly where to find your final materials in the designated class sharing space.

Include a clear **“Start Here” slide or video screen** showing:

- Your team name and topic.
    
- The location of the [learning materials](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/folder/view.php?id=542344 "Learning materials").
    
- The recommended first resource or activity.
    
- Any essential prerequisites or setup instructions.
    

A QR code or clickable link can help, but also provide a readable location or navigation instruction. Check that classmates can access the materials with the intended permissions.

**Submitting files for grading does not automatically make them accessible to classmates.** Make sure the learner-facing materials are available in the designated sharing space before the showcase. Keep personal logs and private team records out of the shared learning resources.

You do not need to build a new learning platform for this assignment.

### E. Follow the Three Rules of Presentation

1. **Cut to the chase.** Quickly establish what classmates can learn and why they should care. Keep introductions and background brief.
    
2. **Show, not tell.** Demonstrate actual materials and learning activities. Replace claims such as “our guide is interactive” with a visible example.
    
3. **Save the best for last.** Finish with a memorable learning result, demonstration, or insight, followed by a clear invitation to try the materials.
    

Design the showcase to grab attention and serve its purpose. Humor, storytelling, a surprising question, or a short challenge can help when they support the learning message.

Maintain the identity of your [learning materials](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/folder/view.php?id=542344 "Learning materials") through consistent language, visuals, and tone. Make text and demonstrations readable for a large classroom audience.

### F. Prepare for Smooth Classroom Playback

**A USB drive containing all required presentation files is mandatory**, whether you present live or use a video.

Before class:

- Copy and test your files on the classroom computer as instructed.
    
- Use clearly named files and have the opening slide or video ready.
    
- Check sound, fonts, visuals, animations, and embedded media.
    
- Prepare an offline fallback for any demonstration that depends on the internet.
    
- Rehearse the complete showcase, including transitions between slides, clips, and demonstrations.
    

Do not rely on downloading files from email, cloud storage, messaging apps, or other online sources during your slot.

For prerecorded showcases:

- Submit an **MP4 video no larger than 250 MB—no exceptions**.
    
- Compress it appropriately while preserving readable screen content and clear audio.
    
- Watch the entire exported file to check timing, synchronization, sound, and image quality.
    
- Correct any captions you include, especially technical terms.
    

### G. Submission and Assessment

Submit **one team submission containing only the files needed to deliver the showcase**:

- **One file:** Submit it directly without a ZIP.
    
- **Multiple files:** Put the required files into one ZIP.
    
- Keep each uploaded file within **250 MB**. Compress media before packaging; ZIP compression is not a substitute for proper video compression.
    

Use a clear filename, such as:

`TeamName_FinalProjectShowcase.pptx`  
`TeamName_FinalProjectShowcase.mp4`  
`TeamName_FinalProjectShowcase.zip`

You do not need to submit a separate written report, script, or timing plan, or upload all your [learning materials](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/folder/view.php?id=542344 "Learning materials") again under this assignment.

Assessment will be based on **the showcase experienced by the class**, whether live or prerecorded, including:

- How clearly it communicates what your team created and its learning value.
    
- How effectively it demonstrates actual materials.
    
- How well it helps classmates find and start using the resources.
    
- Audience engagement, clarity, readability, and sound.
    
- Preparation and compliance with the time limit.
    

The showcase is assessed separately from the quality of the three main deliverables. Production complexity, video length, and the number of presenters do not earn credit by themselves.
