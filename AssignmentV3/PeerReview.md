## Peer Review — Evaluate Two Teams’ Stage 2 [Learning Materials](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/folder/view.php?id=544488 "Learning materials")

**1. General Instructions**

**Deadline: November 17, 2026, at 23:59 Beijing time.** By this deadline, submit your report and share your feedback with both teams so they can use it to improve their final submissions before November 28.

Your team will evaluate **two other teams’ complete Stage 2 submissions** for the three major deliverables: **Study Guide and Test Bank V1.0, PPT Slides V1.0, and Video Tutorials V1.0**.

Approach the task from two perspectives. **As a learner**, use the materials from beginning to end. What can you now explain or do? Where did you get stuck? **As if you were the professor**, judge the quality of the complete work against the published rubrics. A polished opening chapter or an excellent first video may deserve praise, but it cannot establish the quality of the rest of the collection.

**Review everything; select examples when writing the report.** You do not need to comment on every page, slide, question, or minute of video. You do need to examine them all before deciding what score the work deserves and which changes would most benefit learners.

The professor will provide **a matching table** identifying your assigned team and **sample peer reviews from previous years**. Use the samples to see how reviewers organize evidence and explain judgments. This year’s instructions, Shared Assignment Rules, and three published rubrics govern your review.

**2. Specific Requirements**

**A. Review two teams and identify the versions you used**

Review **one team assigned to you in the professor’s matching table** and **one other team of your choice** from Lexue. Find their Stage 2 materials in their **[Learning Center](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/forum/view.php?id=544487 "Learning Center")** threads. At the start of each review, identify the team, its topic, and the V1.0 filenames or posting dates so the authors know exactly which versions your comments refer to.

|**Deliverable**|**What to do as a reviewer**|
|---|---|
|**Study Guide and Test Bank V1.0**|Read the **entire guide** in its intended order. Follow its explanations and examples, work through its activities, and try the resources it asks learners to use. Examine **every test-bank question and its answer or marking guidance**: solve the questions before consulting the key and check whether the answers are accurate, unambiguous, and adequately explained. Judge coverage across the whole module: what is taught well, what is missing, and whether the exercises and questions assess what the guide teaches.|
|**PPT Slides V1.0**|View **every slide in slideshow mode** and read the presenter notes for the whole deck. Follow the lesson from opening to conclusion. Check explanations, diagrams, demonstrations, questions, and answers in context. Judge whether a prepared teammate could teach a coherent lesson from the deck and whether students at the back of a large classroom could follow it.|
|**Video Tutorials V1.0**|Watch **every submitted tutorial from beginning to end**, in the recommended order. Follow the demonstrations and try the learning prompts. Judge coverage, accuracy, pacing, narration, and readability across the collection. Identify where video makes an idea easier to understand and where repetition, slide reading, or an unexplained step gets in the way.|

You may divide the work among team members. **Full coverage is a team responsibility.** Before scoring, bring your findings together: a reviewer who found a clear explanation in the guide may notice that the slides contradict it or that the video assumes knowledge the guide never introduces.

If a required file is missing or inaccessible, contact the team promptly. If the issue remains unresolved, identify exactly what you could not inspect and how that limits your assessment.

**B. Score each deliverable and support your judgments with screenshots**

Use the corresponding published rubric for each deliverable. Give **three separate scores per team, six scores in total**. Show the points awarded for each criterion, the points available, and the total. Report any Study Guide bonus separately. Apply the same standards to both teams.

**Include readable screenshots for each of the three deliverables in each team’s review.** Choose evidence that helps the authors recognize a strength or locate a problem worth fixing. Place each screenshot **beside or immediately below the comment it supports**. Crop or annotate it when that makes the point easier to see.

Identify the location precisely: **guide page or section, test-bank question number, slide number, or video filename and timestamp**. For an issue involving sound, pacing, or a transition, give the relevant time range and describe what happens there; a still image alone cannot show it.

Explain how each example supports your judgment. If a problem occurs throughout a deliverable, show one clear instance and identify other locations where it recurs. If it occurs only once, say so.

**C. Give feedback the authors can use**

For each deliverable, identify a **specific strength worth preserving** and explain why it helps learners. Then identify the most important weakness or opportunity for improvement. A useful comment tells the authors **where to look, what happened when you used the material, and what realistic change would help**.

For example:

**Study Guide §2.3, river-crossing activity:** Asking us to choose the first crossing before revealing the solution made us think about the constraints. Keep that prediction step. As the screenshot below shows, choosing the wolf produces only “Incorrect.” Show the learner which two items remain together on the starting bank—the goat and cabbage—and invite a second attempt. That explains why the move fails without giving away the solution.

For each team, also explain **how well the three deliverables work together**. Trace one important concept across them: where is it introduced, where can learners see it in action, and where can they practise or check their understanding? Point out useful connections as well as gaps or contradictions.

End each team’s review with **two or three revision priorities in order of importance**. Give priority to changes that will make the greatest difference before November 28. Keep the tone professional, direct, and respectful. Address the work and its effect on learners; avoid guessing about the authors’ effort or intentions.

**D. Submit one report and deliver the feedback to both teams**

Submit **one Word or PDF document without a ZIP**, named clearly, for example TeamName_Stage3_PeerReview.pdf. Identify your reviewing team and members. Organize the report under two headings—**Assigned Team** and **Team of Our Choice**—and label the three deliverables within each review. Use consistent formatting and proofread the text and captions.
