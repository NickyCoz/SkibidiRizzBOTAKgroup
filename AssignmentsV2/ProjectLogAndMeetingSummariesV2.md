**C. Record Meetings and Significant Decisions**

Include concise summaries of substantive team meetings and relevant discussions with the teacher.

Record:

· **Date, time, and location or platform.**

· **Attendees**, noting absent members where relevant.

· **Purpose or agenda.**

· **Important discussion points or alternatives considered.**

· **Decisions and outcomes**, including the reasoning where useful.

· **Follow-up actions**, with responsible members and deadlines.

Follow up on earlier agreed actions: identify what was completed, delayed, or revised.

Significant decisions made through group chat may be recorded as dated discussion summaries. A chat screenshot or meeting photograph does not replace a readable summary.

**D. Place Evidence Beside or Below Each Entry**

**Place supporting evidence directly beside or immediately below the relevant entry**, so the explanation and evidence reinforce each other. Do not collect evidence in a separate section or appendix.

This follows the design principle of **proximity**: related information should be close enough for readers to recognize its connection.

Useful Stage 2 evidence includes:

· Before-and-after excerpts showing substantive revisions.

· A tested activity and the result.

· A corrected question with its checked answer and reasoning.

· A slide and relevant presenter notes.

· A meaningful GitHub change showing what was revised and by whom.

· Video export details showing duration and file size, accompanied by observations from watching the compressed version.

· Results of checking a notebook, program, quiz, or website from a learner’s account.

· Feedback and the corresponding improvement.

Use readable screenshots or excerpts with short captions explaining what they demonstrate.

**Evidence of an outcome is more useful than an unsupported claim of effort.** A screenshot of a video editor does not establish video quality, and a file-size screenshot does not establish that the compressed video remains readable.

Include enough evidence to assess the claim without opening external links. You do not need to reproduce entire deliverables inside the log.

**E. Record All Significant AI Use**

Clearly label AI-assisted work within the relevant log entries. Include:

1. **Date, members, and tool:** Who used which AI tool, and when?

2. **Purpose:** What were you trying to understand, create, improve, or solve?

3. **Important interactions:** Relevant prompts, outputs, and follow-up exchanges.

4. **Verification and judgment:** How did you check the output? What did you accept, revise, or reject, and why?

5. **Outcome and learning:** Where was the result used, and what did the team contribute or learn?

Place the interaction excerpts and verification evidence **beside or below the entry**. Related exchanges for one task may be grouped together.

Stage 2 examples may include AI assistance with explanations, test questions, illustrations, scripts, code, narration, or substantial revision.

**A polished AI-generated result, a tool name, or a conversation link is insufficient evidence.** Show the team’s evaluation and decisions. You do not need to make unnecessary changes to correct output merely to demonstrate effort.

Each involved member must also explain their own role and learning in their personal log. Fuller shared evidence may remain in this project log, but the personal entry must contain enough selected evidence to support the individual’s claim.

Missing or inadequate AI-use records will make affected claims of work or contribution **insufficiently supported** and reduce the relevant assessment scores under the Shared Assignment Rules. Never fabricate interactions, checks, or reflections.
