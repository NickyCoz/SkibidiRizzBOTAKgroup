## Study Guide and Test Bank Assessment Rubric

Stage 2 — V1.0

**1. Purpose and Scoring**

Your Study Guide should help beginners **understand, experiment, practise, and enjoy learning**. It should provide a coherent learning experience that students can follow independently.

This rubric assesses the submitted Study Guide and Test Bank together. Read it alongside the assignment instructions and Shared Assignment Rules.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Assessment dimension**|**Maximum points**|
|1. Coverage and learning structure|20|
|2. Accuracy and beginner-friendly explanations|20|
|3. Activities, resources, and learning support|20|
|4. Test Bank and answer quality|20|
|5. Readability and media design|10|
|6. Engagement and consistent identity|10|
|**Base score**|**100**|
|**Optional learning supplements — bonus**|**Up to 10**|

**All 100 base points are available without building your own software, website, or online platform.** Meaningful activities are required, but they can use simple materials or existing tools.

For each dimension, the teacher selects the best-fitting performance level and awards points within its range.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Percentage of the dimension’s available points**|
|**Outstanding**|90–100%|
|**Excellent**|75–89%|
|**Good**|60–74%|
|**Fair**|40–59%|
|**Poor**|1–39%|
|**Missing or no assessable evidence**|0%|

For example, an 80% rating in a 20-point dimension earns **16 points**. The base total is rounded to the nearest whole point.

**2. Assessment Criteria**

**Criterion 1 — Coverage and Learning Structure: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the guide covers the agreed module and gives learners a clear, usable route through it.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|All core topics are developed at an appropriate depth. Learning goals, prerequisites, explanations, activities, and review form a coherent sequence. Learners can readily understand where to begin, what to do next, and how sections connect.|
|**Excellent**|Core coverage is substantially complete and logically organized. Goals and navigation are clear. Only minor gaps or transitions need improvement.|
|**Good**|Most core topics are developed. The sequence is generally usable, but some sections are thin, prerequisites are unclear, or connections need strengthening.|
|**Fair**|Several important topics or required guide components are incomplete. The learning sequence is uneven, and learners must work out some missing connections themselves.|
|**Poor**|Major gaps, placeholders, or disconnected sections prevent meaningful independent use. Learning goals and the intended route are unclear.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Table of contents, learning goals, topic coverage, prerequisites, section sequence, summaries, and glossary.

**Criterion 2 — Accuracy and Beginner-Friendly Explanations: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the guide teaches the ideas correctly and makes them understandable to beginners.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Core explanations and examples are accurate, clear, and carefully developed. Difficult ideas are broken into manageable steps. Likely misconceptions are addressed, and analogies or simplified models have their limits explained. Sources are traceable and appropriate.|
|**Excellent**|Explanations are accurate and accessible, with effective examples and suitable sources. A few minor clarifications would improve the material, but they do not obstruct learning.|
|**Good**|Content is mostly correct and understandable. Some jargon, skipped steps, weak examples, or unsupported claims require attention.|
|**Fair**|Recurring inaccuracies, unexplained terms, or confusing examples make learning difficult. Some claims lack reliable support.|
|**Poor**|Fundamental errors or explanations that learners cannot follow undermine the guide. Important claims and borrowed material are poorly supported or attributed.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Definitions, worked examples, diagrams, calculations, code explanations, source attribution, and treatment of misconceptions.

**Important:** Attractive presentation cannot compensate for teaching a concept incorrectly.

**Criterion 3 — Activities, Resources, and Learning Support: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether learners actively use the ideas and receive enough guidance and feedback to learn from the experience.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Activities are integrated throughout the learning sequence. Learners predict, experiment, solve, build, or explain, then connect results to the concept. Instructions, hints, and feedback support independent progress. Selected platforms and media have a clear purpose and useful introductions.|
|**Excellent**|Activities meaningfully support the main topics and work with the supplied instructions. Reflection and feedback are useful. Linked resources are well chosen and explained, with only minor weaknesses.|
|**Good**|Relevant activities are present and mostly usable, but some emphasize following steps rather than reasoning. Feedback, reflection, or resource introductions need development.|
|**Fair**|Activities are limited, repetitive, or loosely connected to the learning goals. Instructions or feedback are incomplete. Resources are often presented as unexplained links.|
|**Poor**|The guide relies almost entirely on passive reading or watching. Activities are absent, unusable, or offer little connection between actions and learning.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Activity goals, instructions, prediction questions, learner decisions, expected outcomes, explanations, resource introductions, and access arrangements.

A paper-based planning puzzle can score as highly as a programmed simulation if it produces an equally effective learning experience. No particular platform is compulsory.

**Criterion 4 — Test Bank and Answer Quality: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether questions assess important learning accurately and help learners understand their mistakes.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Meets the required question quantities and covers core topics in a balanced way. Questions assess understanding and application as well as essential knowledge. Wording is precise, alternatives are plausible, and open-ended questions require developed reasoning. Answers and explanations are accurate and educationally useful.|
|**Excellent**|Meets the required quantities, with strong coverage and clear questions. Answers and explanations are reliable. Only minor improvements to balance, wording, or depth are needed.|
|**Good**|Provides a broadly useful question set, but has limited coverage gaps, some repetition, weak alternatives, thin explanations, or a modest quantity shortfall. Open-ended questions include meaningful reasoning.|
|**Fair**|Noticeable quantity or coverage shortfalls, excessive recall, ambiguous wording, or unreliable answers reduce usefulness. Several open-ended questions require only brief definitions.|
|**Poor**|Much of the Test Bank is missing, repetitive, trivial, confusing, or incorrect. Answers are absent or too unreliable to support independent practice.|

**Required minimum:** 20 True/False, 20 Multiple Choice, and 6 substantive open-ended questions.

**Evidence to inspect:** Question distribution, numbering, topic alignment, instructions, answer key, reasoning, and expected points for open-ended responses.

Meeting the minimum quantities establishes completeness; it does not guarantee a high score. Quantity shortfalls are assessed here rather than deducted again under Study Guide coverage.

**Criterion 5 — Readability and Media Design: 10 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the layout and media make the guide easy to navigate, understand, and use.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Clear visual hierarchy, readable typography, purposeful visuals, and consistent formatting support learning throughout. Related information is placed together. Code, tables, captions, navigation, and links are easy to use.|
|**Excellent**|The guide is readable, organized, and visually consistent. Media clarify the content. Only minor layout or navigation issues remain.|
|**Good**|Generally readable and usable, but some dense passages, small screenshots, inconsistent formatting, or awkward placement interrupt the experience.|
|**Fair**|Frequent clutter, weak contrast, inconsistent headings, or poorly placed media make reading and navigation unnecessarily difficult.|
|**Poor**|Serious readability or layout problems obstruct use. Important images, code, tables, or instructions are illegible, missing, or broken.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Proximity, alignment, repetition, contrast, heading hierarchy, figure placement, image quality, and document navigation.

Assessment concerns the effectiveness of the design, not the use of expensive software or elaborate decoration.

**Criterion 6 — Engagement and Consistent Identity: 10 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the guide invites learners to continue and feels like one deliberately designed learning experience.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|The guide sustains curiosity through well-chosen challenges, examples, storytelling, humor, or other engaging approaches. Its tone, visuals, recurring elements, and activity design create a recognizable identity that supports understanding.|
|**Excellent**|The guide is engaging and has a clear, consistent identity. Creative elements usually serve the learning goals, with few distractions.|
|**Good**|Some sections are engaging, but the experience is uneven. The tone or visual identity is recognizable but not consistently maintained.|
|**Fair**|Engagement is occasional or superficial. Jokes, memes, stories, or visual styles feel disconnected from one another or from the learning.|
|**Poor**|The guide offers little motivation to continue, or distracting entertainment overwhelms its educational purpose. It feels like unrelated materials assembled together.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Opening challenges, tone of voice, recurring characters or themes, activity labels, learner feedback, and consistency with related slides and videos.

**Humor is one option, not a quota.** Curiosity, discovery, useful challenges, and satisfying feedback can also make learning enjoyable.

**3. Optional Learning Supplements — Up to 10 Bonus Points**

Bonus credit recognizes additional educational value beyond the required guide and activities.

Eligible work may include a thoughtfully extended activity using an existing platform, an online quiz, a notebook, an application, a simulation, a game, or a student-created learning website.

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Bonus criterion**|**Points**|**What earns credit**|
|**Added learning value**|0–4|Addresses a clear learning need and enables useful exploration, practice, or feedback beyond the required work.|
|**Functionality and usability**|0–3|Works reliably, has suitable access settings, and includes clear setup and learner instructions.|
|**Integration and demonstrated contribution**|0–3|Fits the guide’s sequence and identity; the team explains and supports its design, adaptation, implementation, and testing.|
|**Maximum bonus**|**10**|Assessed across the supplements as a whole.|

- More supplements do not automatically earn more points.
- A working link alone does not demonstrate educational design.
- Borrowed tools and materials must be acknowledged.
- Ordinary required activities are assessed in Criterion 3; the bonus recognizes identifiable additional value.
- Optional supplements do not excuse missing core content.
- A supplement whose operation cannot be demonstrated cannot earn functionality points.

**4. Evidence and Fair Assessment**

**Self-contained core submission:** The Study Guide and Test Bank must be submitted as actual files. Permitted links to activities and supplements must follow the assignment’s access requirements.

**Accuracy before polish:** Errors are assessed according to their seriousness and effect on learning. A fundamental misconception weighs more heavily than an isolated typo.

**AI use:** Responsible AI use does not itself reduce the score. Significant use must be documented in the project and personal logs, with evidence beside or below the relevant entry. Missing records affect the relevant process or contribution assessment under the Shared Assignment Rules; incorrect or unusable output is assessed under the applicable product criterion.

**Avoid duplicate penalties:** A shortcoming should be assessed primarily under the criterion it affects. Separate consequences may be considered when they are distinct—for example, missing AI documentation and an incorrect explanation.

**V1.0 expectations:** Minor refinements may remain, but the submitted guide and Test Bank must already be usable. Planned features receive no credit until implemented.
