# Video Tutorials Assessment Rubric

**Stage 2 — V1.0**

**1. Purpose and Scoring**

Your videos should help beginners understand the module through **clear explanations, useful demonstrations, and an engaging viewing experience**.

They should make purposeful use of narration, visuals, timing, and creativity, while reinforcing the Study Guide and PPT Slides.

**Quality matters more than quantity.** More videos, longer runtime, elaborate effects, or more hours spent recording do not automatically earn higher marks.

This rubric assesses the **finished, compressed videos submitted for assessment**, supported by the tutorial guide and required activity files. Scripts and outlines help explain your design but cannot substitute for effective videos.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Assessment dimension**|**Maximum points**|
|1. Essential coverage and tutorial structure|20|
|2. Accuracy and explanatory quality|20|
|3. Audiovisual design and technical quality|20|
|4. Engagement, creativity, and pacing|15|
|5. Active learning and demonstrations|15|
|6. Coherence across the learning system|10|
|**Total**|**100**|

For each dimension, the teacher selects the best-fitting performance level and awards points within its range.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Percentage of the dimension’s available points**|
|**Outstanding**|90–100%|
|**Excellent**|75–89%|
|**Good**|60–74%|
|**Fair**|40–59%|
|**Poor**|1–39%|
|**Missing or no assessable evidence**|0%|

For example, an 80% rating in a 20-point dimension earns **16 points**. The total is rounded to the nearest whole point.

**Submission Limits**

- Submit **1–8 videos in total**.
- Each video must be an **MP4 file no larger than 250 MB, with no exceptions**.
- Upload videos individually through Lexue.
- Links and ZIP archives cannot replace the required videos or bypass the file-size limit.

These are submission requirements, not optional quality targets. Check them before uploading.

**2. Assessment Criteria**

**Criterion 1 — Essential Coverage and Tutorial Structure: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the collection provides a connected route through the module’s essential ideas, with purposeful individual lessons.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Essential ideas are covered at appropriate depth in a coherent sequence. Each video has a clear purpose, introduces or identifies prerequisites, develops its explanation logically, and concludes meaningfully. Related ideas are grouped effectively without unnecessary fragmentation or repetition.|
|**Excellent**|The collection covers the essential ideas and is easy to follow. Individual videos have clear purposes and useful conclusions. Only minor transitions or choices of emphasis need improvement.|
|**Good**|Most essential ideas are covered, but some connections, prerequisites, or conclusions need strengthening. A few videos overlap unnecessarily or have an uneven scope.|
|**Fair**|Important gaps, abrupt jumps, repetition, or fragmented treatment make the collection difficult to follow. Some videos have unclear purposes or incomplete explanations.|
|**Poor**|Major omissions or disconnected recordings prevent beginners from developing a coherent understanding of the module.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Video titles, viewing order, introductions, prerequisites, section transitions, conclusions, and chapter timestamps where needed.

One well-designed video can score as highly as a series. Eight videos is a maximum, not a target.

**Criterion 2 — Accuracy and Explanatory Quality: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the videos explain concepts correctly and help beginners understand the reasoning.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Explanations, examples, and demonstrated results are accurate and accessible. Narration adds reasoning and insight beyond visible text. Difficult ideas are developed through concrete examples, with misconceptions and limits of analogies addressed where relevant.|
|**Excellent**|Explanations are accurate, clear, and supported by effective examples. Narration meaningfully develops the on-screen material. Only minor clarifications are needed.|
|**Good**|Content is mostly correct and understandable, but some jargon, skipped steps, weak examples, or sections of slide-reading limit its effectiveness.|
|**Fair**|Recurring inaccuracies or unclear reasoning obstruct understanding. Narration frequently repeats slide text without explaining the underlying ideas.|
|**Poor**|Fundamental errors undermine the lesson, or the video consists largely of reading text aloud with little meaningful explanation.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Narration, worked examples, diagrams, calculations, code explanations, source attribution, and interpretation of results.

**Reading slides is not effective teaching.** Briefly reading a definition or question is acceptable when followed by explanation or application.

**Criterion 3 — Audiovisual Design and Technical Quality: 20 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the final compressed files communicate clearly through coordinated sound and visuals.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Speech is consistently intelligible, visuals are readable, and narration is synchronized with the relevant actions or diagrams. Highlighting, framing, and progressive explanation guide attention. Compression produces manageable files while preserving all details needed for learning. Captions, where used or needed, are accurate and unobtrusive.|
|**Excellent**|Audio and visuals are clear and well coordinated. Export and compression settings preserve teaching quality. Minor sound, framing, caption, or editing issues do not interfere with learning.|
|**Good**|The videos are generally usable, but occasional small labels, uneven volume, visual–narration mismatches, or compression artifacts require extra effort from viewers.|
|**Fair**|Frequent audio, readability, synchronization, or compression problems interrupt learning. Important details are sometimes difficult to hear or see.|
|**Poor**|Serious sound or image problems make substantial parts difficult to use. Important text, demonstrations, or narration cannot be followed reliably.|

**Evidence to inspect:** The final MP4 files, especially detailed screenshots, moving demonstrations, code, captions, volume consistency, and playback.

Meeting the 250 MB limit alone does not establish good compression. A smaller file is not better if it destroys readability; a larger file earns no advantage if it adds no useful quality.

Professional equipment, on-camera appearances, and a particular resolution are not prerequisites for a high score.

**Criterion 4 — Engagement, Creativity, and Pacing: 15 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the videos sustain attention and use viewers’ time well.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Meaningful openings, purposeful storytelling or other creative choices, expressive delivery, and well-controlled pacing sustain curiosity. Editing removes unnecessary material while preserving thinking time. The ending provides a satisfying educational payoff.|
|**Excellent**|Videos are engaging and efficiently edited. Delivery and creative elements support the learning, with only occasional unnecessary passages or uneven pacing.|
|**Good**|Some sections hold attention well, but repetition, flat delivery, overlong introductions, or uneven pacing weaken the overall experience.|
|**Fair**|Long stretches are slow, repetitive, rushed, or unengaging. Humor, effects, or dramatic elements have little connection to the learning goal.|
|**Poor**|Unedited recording, prolonged slide-reading, excessive filler, or distracting entertainment makes the videos difficult to stay with and learn from.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Opening hooks, narration, narrative development, editing decisions, pacing around difficult ideas, and conclusions.

Apply the three presentation rules:

- **Cut to the chase:** Reach meaningful content promptly.
- **Show, not tell:** Make ideas visible through examples and demonstrations.
- **Save the best for last:** Build toward a useful application or insight.

Humor and drama are options, not quotas. Curiosity, surprise, discovery, and a sense of achievement can also make a tutorial compelling.

A longer video can earn full marks when its content and pacing justify the duration. Shortness alone does not establish quality.

**Criterion 5 — Active Learning and Demonstrations: 15 Points**

**What matters:** Whether viewers are helped to think, practise, and interpret what they see.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|Demonstrations reveal both what happens and why. Viewers are invited to predict, solve, modify, or explain at meaningful points, with sufficient instructions and useful feedback. Starting conditions and important settings are clear, allowing learners to reproduce or extend relevant activities.|
|**Excellent**|Demonstrations and learning checks are purposeful and usable. Instructions and feedback support understanding, with only minor gaps.|
|**Good**|Relevant demonstrations or practice prompts are present, but some lack interpretation, sufficient guidance, or developed feedback.|
|**Fair**|Viewers mainly watch procedures without understanding the reasoning. Questions are superficial, answers appear too soon, or activities lack information needed to attempt them.|
|**Poor**|Meaningful learning checks and demonstrations are absent or unusable. Viewers cannot connect the shown actions to the intended concepts.|

**Evidence to inspect:** Prediction prompts, pause-and-try tasks, demonstration setup, reasoning, answer explanations, and supporting activity files.

An algorithm trace or paper-based puzzle can be as effective as a software demonstration. The value lies in what learners do and understand.

**Criterion 6 — Coherence Across the Learning System: 10 Points**

**What matters:** Whether the videos reinforce the Study Guide and slides while contributing something useful through video.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Level**|**Description**|
|**Outstanding**|The resources form a unified learning experience. Concepts, terminology, examples, and answers agree. Specific references connect viewing to practice and review. A recognizable identity is maintained, while video adds useful explanation, movement, demonstration, or emotional engagement.|
|**Excellent**|Videos are consistent with the other resources and make useful connections to them. Their role and shared identity are clear, with minor mismatches only.|
|**Good**|Resources are broadly consistent, but connections are generic or limited. Some material is repeated without taking advantage of video.|
|**Fair**|Inconsistent terminology, examples, style, or references make movement between resources confusing. The videos contribute little beyond duplicating existing material.|
|**Poor**|Significant contradictions or disconnected design prevent the resources from functioning as a coherent learning system.|

**Evidence to inspect:** References to guide sections and questions, shared examples, supporting files, tutorial-guide navigation, terminology, tone, and visual identity.

Coherence means more than matching colors. Learners should understand how watching, reading, practising, and checking their understanding fit together.

**3. Evidence and Fair Assessment**

**Judge the complete collection.** Assessment considers the submitted videos as a whole. One polished introduction or impressive demonstration does not compensate for consistently weak teaching elsewhere.

**Judge the final compressed version.** Clear original recordings do not compensate for unreadable or inaudible exports.

**V1.0 must already teach effectively.** Minor refinements may remain, but planned videos, unrecorded scripts, and promised improvements receive no credit until implemented.

**Quality outweighs quantity.** There are no automatic rewards for producing more videos, increasing runtime, or reporting more production hours.

**Supporting documents cannot replace the viewing experience.** The tutorial guide and scripts support navigation and assessment. Essential explanations must appear in the videos where viewers need them.

**AI use does not itself reduce the score.** Significant assistance must be documented in the project and personal logs, with evidence beside or below the relevant entry. Missing documentation affects the relevant process or contribution assessment under the Shared Assignment Rules. Errors or ineffective narration are assessed under the applicable product criterion, regardless of how they were produced.

**Avoid duplicate penalties.** Assess a shortcoming primarily under the criterion it affects. Distinct consequences may be considered separately—for example, an incorrect explanation and an unreadable demonstration.
