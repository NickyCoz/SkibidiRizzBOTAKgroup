**2. Specific Requirements**

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **November 7, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**

**A. Complete the Core Content**

Develop all core topics within your module’s agreed scope. Check the coverage against your learning goals and the course materials.

Include:

· A clear module overview and learning outcomes.

· A logical sequence of topics and subtopics.

· Explanations of essential concepts and unfamiliar terminology.

· Worked examples or demonstrations for the main ideas.

· Diagrams, tables, or other visuals where they improve understanding.

· Meaningful learner activities or application questions.

· Checked answers, expected outcomes, or explanations.

· References and attribution for borrowed material.

**Core sections must contain actual learning content, not headings, brief placeholders, or promises of future work.**

Optional extensions may remain unfinished if clearly labelled. Identify any remaining core gaps honestly and explain how you will resolve them; labelling a gap does not make the core content complete.

There is no fixed page or word count. The appropriate length depends on what learners need to understand and practise.

**B. Strengthen the Learning Sequence**

Review the module from a beginner’s perspective.

· Introduce prerequisite ideas before using them.

· Explain how related concepts connect.

· Break difficult processes into understandable steps.

· Use concrete examples to clarify abstract ideas.

· Address likely misunderstandings where relevant.

· Give learners opportunities to apply ideas and check their understanding.

For each major topic, include a suitable example or demonstration and an opportunity to apply the idea. A well-designed activity may cover several connected concepts.

Avoid relying entirely on definitions and recall questions. Include tasks such as tracing a process, predicting an outcome, comparing approaches, explaining a decision, or finding an error.

**Include the instructions, information, and answers needed to use the material.** External links may support further exploration but must not replace essential explanations or activities.

**C. Revise Using Feedback**

Address relevant teacher feedback, status-report feedback, and any general feedback provided for previous assignments. Check whether comments about one section reveal similar problems elsewhere.

Include a concise **Revision Summary** in the submission report:

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Feedback or problem**|**What changed and why**|**Where to find the change**|
|A specific issue identified through feedback or review|The correction or improvement made|Markdown filename and section heading|

Prioritize substantive improvements, such as correcting a misconception, developing a missing topic, improving an example, or making an activity usable.

If a relevant suggestion is not adopted, briefly explain why. If no teacher feedback is available, document improvements arising from your own review and testing.

Submit the **complete updated content**, not only the changed sections.

**D. Check Accuracy and Usability**

Have members review one another’s content. Each core section should be checked by someone other than its original author.

Check that:

· Important claims agree with reliable sources.

· Examples, calculations, code, and answers are correct.

· Activities can be completed using the supplied instructions and information.

· Terminology is consistent throughout the module.

· References exist and support the relevant claims.

· Images and local file references work in the submitted copy.

Ask a teammate who did not write a selected explanation or activity to follow it as a learner. Record what they understood, where they became confused, and what you changed.

Keep review and testing evidence in the **Stage 2 project and personal logs**, directly beside or below the relevant entries. In this assignment’s report, briefly summarize the checks and identify any unresolved problems.

When an error is found during production, correct the Markdown source and every affected resource. Do not leave conflicting definitions, examples, or answers across versions.
