## Personal Learning and Work Log V2(15XP)

**Opened:** 星期一, 12 十月 2026, 12:14 AM

**Due:** 星期六, 7 十一月 2026, 11:54 PM

**1. General Instructions**

Continue recording your individual learning and work as your team develops its [learning materials](https://lexue.bit.edu.cn/mod/folder/view.php?id=544488 "Learning materials") and prepares or delivers its mini lecture.

Your log should show **what you personally contributed, what you learned, how you checked your understanding, and how you responded to difficulties and feedback**.

Cover both your project work and your learning of the wider course content. **A production role does not remove your responsibility to learn CS and AI.** For example, editing videos or formatting slides should involve understanding the content you are presenting.

**Every student must write their own log.** The project log records the team’s development; your personal log explains your own actions, decisions, and learning.

Read the **Shared Assignment Rules** and any feedback on your Stage 1 log. The quality checklist is included below.

**2. Specific Requirements**

**Assignment type:** Individual work, collected into one team submission  
**Deadline:** **November 7, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**  
**Period covered:** **October 25–November 7, 2026**

Submit **Stage 2 entries only**. You may briefly refer to an earlier difficulty or goal when explaining progress, but do not include your full Stage 1 log again.

**A. Keep Dated, Specific Entries**

Write entries as meaningful learning or work takes place. Include the date and, where useful, the time. No empty entries are needed for days without activity.

Each entry should explain:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Item**|**What to record**|
|**Activity and purpose**|What did you study, practise, create, check, or improve? Why?|
|**Your own contribution**|What did you personally do? Distinguish your role from teammates’ work and AI assistance.|
|**Outcome and evidence**|What resulted? Place supporting evidence beside or below the entry.|
|**Learning and reflection**|What changed in your understanding or ability? What difficulty, mistake, or uncertainty did you encounter?|
|**Next step**|What will you investigate, practise, or improve next, where relevant?|

Keep routine entries brief. Give more detail to important learning, decisions, and problem-solving.

A general statement such as “I worked on the videos for three hours” is insufficient. Explain which part you worked on, what you changed, and how you judged the result.

**B. Show Both Contribution and Learning**

Record the activities you actually undertake. Relevant areas include:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Area**|**What your entry could demonstrate**|
|**Subject understanding**|How you clarified a concept, corrected a misconception, solved a problem, or checked an explanation.|
|**Learning-material production**|Your contribution to Markdown, the Study Guide, Test Bank, slides, videos, or optional supplements.|
|**Review and testing**|A question you verified, an activity you tested, or a problem you identified in another member’s work.|
|**Design and communication**|Why you changed an explanation, visual, activity, or video sequence to help beginners learn.|
|**Tools and techniques**|What you learned through GitHub, Markdown, recording, editing, compression, programming, or another relevant tool.|
|**Teamwork and feedback**|How you coordinated work, responded to feedback, resolved a difficulty, or helped improve a shared decision.|
|**Mini lectures**|Your preparation, rehearsal, delivery, or learning from other teams’ lectures, where these have occurred.|

You do not need to cover every category in every entry. The log should reflect your actual work rather than a manufactured list of activities.

**Show what you learned through production.** For example, revising a diagram should involve checking what it represents, not only changing its appearance.

**C. Continue Learning Beyond Your Team’s Module**

Include specific examples of learning from class, course materials, other teams’ resources, and independent practice.

Do more than list the topics you encountered. Show how you engaged with them, such as:

· Explaining a concept in your own words.

· Solving an exercise and checking the reasoning.

· Tracing an algorithm or program.

· Testing a prediction in an interactive platform.

· Comparing two approaches.

· Investigating a question you could not initially answer.

For mini lectures you have attended, record a useful idea, remaining question, or application you explored. Merely copying the presenter’s slides does not demonstrate learning.

Do not record lectures or activities that have not yet taken place.

**D. Place Evidence Beside or Below Each Entry**

**Place supporting evidence directly beside or immediately below the relevant entry**, so that the explanation and evidence reinforce each other. Do not collect evidence in a separate section or appendix.

This follows the design principle of **proximity**: related information should be placed close together so readers can recognize the connection easily.

Suitable evidence includes:

· Your worked solution, annotated diagram, or explanation.

· Before-and-after excerpts showing a meaningful correction.

· A GitHub change showing your actual contribution.

· A question you checked and the reasoning behind its answer.

· Test results from an activity, program, or notebook.

· A revised slide and an explanation of the design decision.

· Video export details accompanied by checks of the final viewing quality.

· Important AI interactions and your verification of the results.

Use readable excerpts or screenshots with short captions. Include enough context to understand what the evidence shows.

For shared work, identify **your contribution**. A screenshot of the team’s finished product does not by itself establish what you did.

You may refer to a project-log entry for fuller team context, but your personal entry must include enough selected evidence to assess your own claim without following external links or searching another document.

**E. Record All Significant AI Use**

Record your significant AI use for **both project work and personal learning**, including uses that did not produce a final deliverable.

Clearly label the relevant entry **AI Use** and include:

1. **Date and tool:** Which AI tool did you use, and when?

2. **Purpose:** What were you trying to understand, create, improve, or solve?

3. **Important interactions:** Relevant prompts, outputs, and follow-up exchanges.

4. **Verification and judgment:** How did you check the response? What did you accept, revise, or reject, and why?

5. **Outcome and learning:** What resulted, what did you contribute, and what can you now explain or do?

Place the interaction and verification evidence **beside or below the entry**. Related exchanges for one task may be grouped together. Routine spelling corrections do not require individual records.

For shared AI work documented in the project log, include the selected evidence needed to explain your role and identify the entry containing the fuller record. Do not duplicate an entire conversation unnecessarily.

**Receiving an explanation from AI is not the same as understanding it.** Show how you checked your learning—for example, by explaining the idea in your own words, solving a new example, or testing a prediction.

If AI substantially helps draft or rewrite your log, document that assistance too. Never use AI to invent experiences, contributions, checks, or reflections.

Missing or inadequate records of significant AI use will make affected claims **insufficiently supported** and reduce the relevant assessment scores under the Shared Assignment Rules.

**F. Reflect on Progress and Feedback**

Use your entries to show how your work and understanding develop.

Where relevant, explain:

· What you changed in response to Stage 1 feedback.

· A mistake you discovered and how you corrected it.

· Why an initial approach was ineffective.

· How reviewing or teaching a concept exposed a gap in your understanding.

· What you can now do independently.

· What still requires practice or help.

Be specific. “I learned teamwork is important” provides little insight unless you explain the situation and what you will do differently.

You do not need to invent a major lesson for every routine task. Honest observations and unresolved questions are useful evidence of learning.

Write in your own voice. Chinese students may write in Chinese or bilingually for clarity.

**G. Submission and Assessment**

Collect all members’ logs into **one Word or PDF document** named:

**TeamName_Stage2_PersonalLogs**

· Include a contents list identifying every member.

· Start each member’s log on a new page.

· Label it with the member’s **name as shown in the class roster, student ID, team name, and reporting period**.

· Keep each person’s entries in chronological order.

· Preserve each student’s own writing and perspective.

· Keep evidence beside or below its relevant entry.

· **Do not ZIP this single document.**

The designated submission lead collects and submits the logs. Each student remains responsible for checking their own section.

Submit through the designated assignment channel. **Do not publish personal logs in the public repository or class resource-sharing space.**

Assessment considers each student’s **documented learning, contribution, evidence, reflection, response to feedback, and clarity**. Page count, screenshots, hours claimed, and polished language do not automatically demonstrate quality.
