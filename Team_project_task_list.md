# 2.Project scope and learning goals
## 2.1
Target Learners Our target learners are begginers studying Computer Science, and Artificial Intelligence who have a basic understanding of computers and introductory programming concepts. We assume that larners are familiar with fundamental programming ideas such as variables, conditional statements, loops, and functions. but may have limited experience with algorithm nalysis, data structures, and neural networks.

The learnign materials will introduce these concepts progressively, using clear explanations, practical examples, visual representations, and simple demonstrations. Advanced mathematical knowldge and prior xperience with machine lerning will not be required.
# 2.2 Learning Goals

After completing our learning materials, learners should be able to:

Explain algorithms and describe how algorithms provide step-by-step solutions to computational problems.

Understand basic algorithm analysis, including the purpose of time complexity and space complexity, and interpret introductory Big-O notation.

Identify and compare fundamental data structures, including arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, hash tables, trees, and graphs, and explain when each may be useful.

Describe common algorithms, such as searching and sorting, and explain their basic operations and differences in efficiency.

Explain the relationship between algorithms and data structures, including how the choice of data structure can affect an algorithm's performance.

Describe connectionist AI, particularly artificial neural networks, and explain how artificial neurons, layers, weights, and activation functions contribute to learning and prediction.

Distinguish traditional algorithmic problem-solving from connectionist approaches, recognizing that traditional algorithms generally follow explicitly specified procedures, whereas neural networks learn patterns from data during training.

Apply introductory concepts to simple problems, select an appropriate data structure or algorithm for a given situation, and explain the reasoning behind the choice.

Interpret simple neural network examples, including how input data passes through a network to produce an output and how training can adjust weights to improve predictions.

# 2.3 Content Outline

Our project will cover three interconnected areas: algorithms, data structures, and connectionist artificial intelligence.

A. Algorithms

Definition and characteristics of algorithms.

Representing algorithms using pseudocode and flowcharts.

Searching algorithms, including linear search and binary search.

Sorting algorithms, including bubble sort and merge sort, with other sorting methods introduced where appropriate.

Introduction to algorithm efficiency, including time complexity, space complexity, and Big-O notation.

Comparing algorithms based on correctness, efficiency, and suitability for different problems.

B. Data Structures

Introduction to data organization and the purpose of data structures.

Arrays and linked lists.

Stacks and queues.

Hash tables and basic hashing concepts.

Trees and their basic terminology.

Graphs and their representations.

Introductory comparisons of data structures based on their operations, advantages, limitations, and common applications.

The relationship between data structures and algorithm efficiency.

C. Connectionist Artificial Intelligence

Introduction to connectionism and its role in artificial intelligence.

Biological inspiration for artificial neural networks.

Artificial neurons, inputs, weights, biases, and activation functions.

Basic neural network architecture, including input, hidden, and output layers.

Forward propagation and the process of generating predictions.

The basic idea of training a neural network using examples and adjusting weights.

Introductory explanations of loss functions, gradient descent, and backpropagation.

Practical applications of neural networks, such as image recognition, text processing, and pattern classification.

D. Connections Between the Three Areas

How algorithms operate on and manipulate data structures.

How the choice of data structure influences computational efficiency.

How algorithms are used to train and run neural networks.

Differences between explicitly programmed procedures and models that learn patterns from data.

Practical examples illustrating how these concepts contribute to solving computational problems.

# 2.4 Scope Boundaries

The project will prioritize conceptual understanding, practical examples, and beginner-friendly explanations rather than exhaustive coverage of every topic.

Topics requiring detailed explanation:

The basic principles and step-by-step operation of searching and sorting algorithms.

Fundamental data structures and their common operations.

Introductory time complexity and Big-O notation.

The relationships between algorithms, data structures, and computational efficiency.

The structure and basic operation of artificial neural networks.

How forward propagation and the general training process work.

Topics receiving a brief introduction:

Advanced algorithm design techniques and formal proofs of correctness.

Complex graph algorithms and specialized data structures.

Advanced mathematical analysis of algorithm complexity.

Detailed neural network mathematics, including extensive calculus and matrix derivations.

Advanced optimization techniques, specialized neural network architectures, and large-scale deep learning systems.

The final level of detail will be adjusted according to the course materials, available time, and the team's understanding of the selected module. The project will emphasize explanations that beginners can understand and apply rather than attempting to cover every advanced topic.
