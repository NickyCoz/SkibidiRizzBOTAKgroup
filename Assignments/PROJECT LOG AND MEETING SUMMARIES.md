## Project log and meeting summaries (15XP)

**Opened:** 星期一, 7 九月 2026, 12:14 AM

**Due:** 星期六, 24 十月 2026, 11:54 PM

**1. General Instructions**

Maintain a clear, authentic record of your team’s work during Stage 1. The document should show **what was done, when, by whom, what resulted, and what the team learned**.

Use the log to coordinate work, identify problems, and improve your approach. Record important meetings and decisions so that members understand their responsibilities and agreed next steps.

**Record the work as it happens.** A general summary written just before the deadline does not replace a chronological project log.

Your records must include evidence of meaningful work, including **all significant AI use affecting the shared project**. A polished final product alone does not demonstrate how it was produced or what your team contributed.

Refer to the **Shared Assignment Rules**. The quality checklist for this assignment is included below.

**2. Specific Requirements**

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **October 24, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**  
**Period covered:** From the beginning of your team’s project work through the Stage 1 deadline.

**A. Project Log**

Keep entries in chronological order, with dates and clear headings. Record each day on which meaningful project work occurs; you do not need empty entries for days without activity.

Each entry should answer the following questions:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Item**|**What to record**|
|**When?**|The date of the work, with a time where useful.|
|**What was done, and by whom?**|Specific tasks and the members responsible. Distinguish completed work from attempts or ongoing work.|
|**What resulted?**|The content, decision, correction, or other outcome, supported by readable evidence.|
|**What was learned?**|A useful insight, difficulty, mistake, or improvement in the team’s understanding or working methods.|
|**What remains unresolved?**|Important questions, problems, or dependencies, where applicable.|
|**What happens next?**|Concrete follow-up tasks, responsible members, and intended completion dates.|

Cover the work actually undertaken, such as planning, research, GitHub setup, Markdown development, content checking, and status-report preparation.

Write specific entries. For example, “Corrected the binary-search example after testing an odd-length list” is more informative than “Worked on algorithms.”

Keep routine entries brief. Give more detail to significant decisions, problems, and learning.

**B. Meeting Summaries**

Include a summary of each substantive project meeting, whether held in person or online, including relevant discussions with the teacher.

Record:

· **Date, time, and location or platform.**

· **Attendees**, noting absent members where relevant.

· **Purpose or agenda:** What needed discussion?

· **Key discussion points:** Important options, questions, or disagreements.

· **Decisions and outcomes:** What was agreed, and why where relevant?

· **Follow-up actions:** What must be done, by whom, and by when?

**A meeting summary is a concise record of decisions and actions, not a transcript.** Screenshots of a chat or a photograph of members meeting do not replace the summary.

If a significant decision is made through an ongoing group chat, record it as a dated discussion summary. Routine messages do not need separate summaries.

**C. Evidence of Work**

Include evidence directly **beside the relevant entry**.

Suitable evidence includes:

· Readable excerpts or screenshots of developed content.

· Before-and-after examples showing a correction or improvement.

· Relevant GitHub changes showing the author, date, and substance of the work.

· Results of testing an example, activity, calculation, or answer.

· Annotated diagrams, review comments, or task-list updates.

Explain briefly **what each item demonstrates**. Use labels such as “Evidence E3” so that entries and contribution claims can refer to it without repeating the same image.

**Prioritize evidence of outcomes.** A photograph of someone working, a chat message saying “finished,” or a commit count does not by itself establish the substance or quality of a contribution.

The document must be self-contained. Repository links and other external links may supplement the evidence, but the teacher should not need to open them to assess your work.

**D. Significant AI Use**

**Record all significant AI use affecting project content, deliverables, or decisions.** This includes AI assistance with research, understanding concepts, planning, writing, code, diagrams, questions, and substantial revision.

You may integrate clearly labelled **AI-use records** into the relevant project-log entries or collect them in a dedicated section with cross-references to those entries. Keep the records inside this document and easy to locate.

For each significant activity, include:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Item**|**Required information**|
|**Date, members, and tool**|Who used which AI tool, and when?|
|**Purpose**|What were you trying to understand, create, improve, or solve?|
|**Important interactions**|Relevant prompts, outputs, and follow-up exchanges, shown through readable excerpts or screenshots.|
|**Verification and judgment**|How was the output checked? What was accepted, revised, or rejected, and why?|
|**Outcome and learning**|Where was the result used? What did the team contribute or learn?|

You may group related exchanges from one task into one record. You do not need to reproduce every message or record routine spelling corrections individually.

**A tool name, a link to a conversation, or an image of the finished AI-generated product is insufficient.** Show enough of the interaction and checking process to make the work understandable and verifiable.

Each involved member must also explain their own role and learning in their personal log. Full shared evidence may appear once in this project log, with a precise reference from the personal log.

Missing or inadequate AI-use records will make the affected claims of work or contribution **insufficiently supported** and will reduce the relevant documentation and/or contribution scores under the Shared Assignment Rules. Never fabricate interactions, verification steps, or reflections.

**E. Team Member Contributions**

At the end of the document, include a contribution table covering **Stage 1 only**.

|   |   |   |   |
|---|---|---|---|
|**Member’s name and student ID**|**Main contributions**|**Relative contribution score**|**Supporting evidence**|
|Name as shown in the class roster; student ID|Specific work and outcomes|Score out of 100|Relevant entry dates, evidence labels, or page numbers|

The highest contributor receives **100**, and other scores indicate contributions relative to that member. Equally strong contributors may receive the same score. **These scores do not need to add up to 100.**

Consider the substance, quality, responsibility, and usefulness of each member’s work. Do not base scores only on hours claimed, pages produced, commit counts, or the number of AI prompts used.

Review the table together. Support the scores honestly with evidence and identify unresolved disagreements if necessary. These are the team’s assessments; the teacher may adjust them based on the available evidence.

**F. Submission**

Submit **one readable Word or PDF document** named:

**TeamName_Stage1_ProjectLog**

Organize it into:

1. Team information and reporting period.

2. Chronological project log.

3. Meeting summaries.

4. AI-use records and supporting evidence, if not integrated into the entries.

5. Stage 1 contribution table.

Include all required evidence in the document. **Do not submit only links, and do not ZIP this single file.**

Use consistent headings, readable screenshots, captions, and page numbers. Avoid dumping full chat histories or large collections of unlabelled images into the document.

The designated submission lead submits one copy after the team has checked it. Continue maintaining the working records after Stage 1.

Assessment will consider the records’ **completeness, authenticity, evidence, useful reflection, contribution transparency, and readability**. Length and visual polish alone do not demonstrate quality.
