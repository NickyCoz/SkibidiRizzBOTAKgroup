## Personal Learning and Work Log (15XP)

**Opened:** 星期一, 7 九月 2026, 12:14 AM

**Due:** 星期六, 24 十月 2026, 11:54 PM

**Stage 1 — Personal Learning and Work Log**

**1. General Instructions**

Keep a personal record of your learning and work throughout Stage 1. Show **what you did, what you learned, how you addressed difficulties, and what evidence supports your progress**.

Your log should cover both your contribution to the team project and your learning of the wider course content. **Your responsibility to learn CS and AI extends beyond your team’s selected module.**

Write in your own voice. Explain your actual experience, including questions, mistakes, discoveries, and changes in understanding. A useful log shows your thinking as well as your activities.

**Every student must write their own log.** The team’s project log does not replace it, and one member must not write everyone’s reflections.

Read the **Shared Assignment Rules**. The quality checklist is integrated into this assignment.

**2. Specific Requirements**

**Assignment type:** Individual work, collected into one team submission  
**Deadline:** **October 24, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**  
**Period covered:** From the beginning of your course learning and project work through the Stage 1 deadline.

**A. Record Your Learning and Work Regularly**

Write dated entries as meaningful learning or work takes place. Include the time where useful. You do not need empty entries for days without activity.

Each entry should explain:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Item**|**What to record**|
|**Activity and purpose**|What did you study, practise, create, check, or improve? Why?|
|**Your own contribution**|What did you personally do? If you worked with others, distinguish your role from theirs.|
|**Outcome and evidence**|What resulted? Place the supporting evidence beside the entry.|
|**Learning and reflection**|What do you now understand or do better? What mistake, difficulty, or uncertainty did you encounter?|
|**Next step**|What will you investigate, practise, or improve next, where relevant?|

Keep routine entries short and give more detail to significant learning or problem-solving. A weekly summary alone does not replace dated records of the work.

Avoid vague statements such as “I learned a lot” or “I helped with Markdown.” Identify the concept, task, change, or result.

**B. Cover Both Project Work and Course Learning**

Your log should reflect the activities you actually undertake, including:

· **Course learning:** Concepts studied in class or independently, questions explored, exercises attempted, and misunderstandings corrected.

· **Project contributions:** Research, planning, writing, examples, diagrams, content checking, or status-report preparation.

· **Tools and working methods:** What you learned through using Markdown, GitHub, or other relevant tools.

· **Teamwork:** Your role in discussions, reviews, decisions, and resolving difficulties.

For learning outside your team’s module, include concrete examples of what you studied and how you checked your understanding. Merely listing lecture titles or copying course notes is insufficient.

You do not need to cover every category in every entry. Across Stage 1, the log should show both individual learning and substantive participation.

**C. Place Evidence Beside or Below Each Entry**

**Place supporting evidence directly beside or immediately below the relevant entry**, so that the explanation and evidence reinforce each other. Do not collect evidence in a separate section or appendix.

This follows an important design principle you will learn later: **proximity**. Related information should be placed close together so that readers can easily recognize the connection without searching elsewhere.

This requirement applies to **both personal logs and project logs**, including AI-use records. Choose a layout that keeps the text and evidence readable; a two-column table is optional.

Suitable evidence includes:

· An excerpt of content you wrote or corrected.

· A worked exercise, calculation, diagram, or explanation in your own words.

· Before-and-after examples showing an improvement.

· A relevant GitHub change showing your actual contribution.

· Test results or checks of an example or answer.

· Important AI interactions together with your verification and decisions.

Crop screenshots to the relevant area and add a short caption explaining what they show. Keep the text readable; use additional rows or pages rather than shrinking evidence to fit.

**Show outcomes and thinking.** A photograph of yourself working, a task marked “done,” or a screenshot of an open application provides little evidence by itself.

Include the evidence needed to assess your personal claim directly beside the entry. You may refer to a project-log entry for fuller team context, but that reference must not replace evidence of your own work.

**D. Record All Significant AI Use**

Record your significant use of AI for both **project work and personal learning**, including uses that did not lead to a final deliverable.

Clearly label the relevant entry **AI Use** and include:

1. **Date and tool:** Which AI tool did you use, and when?

2. **Purpose:** What were you trying to understand, create, improve, or solve?

3. **Important interactions:** Relevant prompts, outputs, and follow-up exchanges.

4. **Verification and judgment:** How did you check the response? What did you accept, revise, or reject, and why?

5. **Outcome and learning:** What resulted, what did you contribute, and what can you now explain or do?

Place the interaction excerpts and verification evidence **beside the entry**. Related exchanges for one task may be grouped together. Routine spelling corrections do not need individual records.

For shared AI work already documented in the project log, include the selected evidence needed to explain **your own role**, and identify the project-log entry containing the fuller record. You do not need to duplicate the entire conversation.

**“AI helped me” is not sufficient.** For example, if AI explained binary search, show how you checked your understanding by tracing a new example yourself.

Do not use AI to invent experiences, reflections, verification steps, or contributions. If AI substantially helps draft or rewrite your log, document that assistance too; the account must remain accurate and personal.

**Missing or inadequate records of significant AI use will make the affected claims of work or learning insufficiently supported and will reduce the relevant assessment scores under the Shared Assignment Rules.**

**E. Write Honestly and Clearly**

Use first-person language and distinguish what you understand from what remains uncertain.

Useful reflection explains a specific change, such as:

· What you initially misunderstood and how you corrected it.

· Why an approach failed and what you tried next.

· How feedback improved your work.

· What you can now do independently and what still requires help.

You do not need to invent a major insight for every routine task. Honest, specific observations are more useful than repeated claims of success.

You may use a personal, engaging style, but elaborate decoration and long narratives are unnecessary. Chinese students may write in Chinese or bilingually for clarity.

**F. Submission and Assessment**

Collect all team members’ logs into **one Word or PDF document** named:

**TeamName_Stage1_PersonalLogs**

· Include a contents list identifying every member.

· Start each member’s log on a new page.

· Label each log with the member’s **name as shown in the class roster, student ID, team name, and reporting period**.

· Keep each person’s entries in chronological order.

· Preserve each student’s own writing and perspective.

· Keep evidence beside its relevant entry throughout the document.

· Submit the actual content and evidence; external links are supplementary only.

· **Do not ZIP this single document.**

The designated submission lead collects and submits the logs. Each student remains responsible for checking their own section.

Submit through the designated assignment channel. **Do not publish personal logs in the public repository or class resource-sharing space.**

Assessment considers each student’s **documented learning, contribution, evidence, reflection, and clarity**. Log length, screenshots alone, or polished language do not establish meaningful learning.
