## Team Project Plan and Task List (20XP)

**Opened:** 星期日, 27 九月 2026, 6:14 PM

**Due:** 星期六, 24 十月 2026, 11:54 PM

**1. General Instructions**

Work together to create a practical plan for your team’s course project. The plan should explain **what you intend to teach, what you will produce, how you will organize the work, and who will be responsible for each task**.

Your team will develop shared content in Markdown and use it to produce three connected learning resources: a **Study Guide, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials**. You will also prepare a live mini lecture on one important CS/AI idea.

Discuss the plan with all members. Base your decisions on the project requirements, your current understanding of the selected module, and the strengths and learning needs of your team.

**This is a working document.** Keep the plan and task list updated as your understanding develops, feedback arrives, and circumstances change.

Read the attached **Shared Assignment Rules**, including the requirements for Markdown, GitHub, contribution evidence, and AI-use records.

**2. Specific Requirements**

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **October 24, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**

**A. Team Information and Responsibilities**

Include:

· Team name and selected project module.

· Each member’s name as shown in the class roster and student ID.

· Each member’s relevant strengths, experience, and skills they need to develop.

· Responsibilities assigned to each member, including coordination and submission.

Explain briefly how your team will use its members’ strengths and distribute the workload fairly.

**Roles describe responsibilities, not exclusive areas of work.** Everyone must study the subject, make a substantive contribution, and understand the team’s core content.

**B. Project Scope and Learning Goals**

Describe:

· **Target learners:** What background knowledge will you assume?

· **Learning goals:** What should beginners be able to explain or do after using your materials?

· **Content outline:** What major topics and subtopics will your project cover?

· **Scope boundaries:** Which ideas need detailed explanation, and which will receive only a brief introduction?

Use the course materials to establish the expected coverage. Identify any uncertainty about scope or difficulty that you need to discuss with the teacher.

The outline should be specific enough to guide research and task allocation. You do not need to write the complete learning content in this assignment.

**C. Planned Learning Resources and Mini Lecture**

Explain your initial plan for:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Component**|**What to describe**|
|**Shared Markdown content**|How you will organize explanations, examples, activities, and references as the foundation for the learning resources.|
|**Study Guide and Test Bank**|The proposed structure and how learners will study, practise, and check their understanding.|
|**PPT Slides**|How you will explain important ideas visually, including possible diagrams or worked examples.|
|**Video Tutorials**|The proposed tutorial topics and which concepts or processes would benefit from narration and demonstration.|

Explain how the three learning resources will support one another. Your mini lecture should focus on one manageable question within the broader project.

These are initial plans and may be revised.

Plan around the following stage deadlines:

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Stage**|**Deadline**|**Main checkpoint**|
|**Stage 1**|**October 24, 2026**|Planning and content foundation|
|**Stage 2**|**November 7, 2026**|First versions and live teaching preparation/delivery according to the class schedule|
|**Stage 3**|**November 28, 2026**|Review, revision, and final delivery|

The dates of live classroom presentations will follow the announced class schedule.

Your plan should explain:

· How members will study and investigate the content.

· How Markdown content will be developed and checked before full production.

· How GitHub will be used to organize work and track contributions.

· How members will communicate, meet, and review one another’s work.

· How corrections will reach every affected deliverable.

· When you will test activities, rehearse presentations, and collect feedback.

Set internal deadlines before the submission deadlines, allowing time for review and correction. Identify the main risks—such as unfamiliar tools, difficult content, uneven workloads, or delayed tasks—and explain how you will respond.

**E. Master Task List**

Create a task list covering the whole project. **Stage 1 tasks should be detailed; later-stage tasks may be broader initially and refined as the project progresses.**

For each task, include:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Field**|**Required information**|
|**Task**|A specific action to complete|
|**Expected result**|The file, content, decision, or other outcome that will show completion|
|**Responsible member(s)**|Who will do the work; identify a lead where several members are involved|
|**Internal deadline**|When the task should be completed|
|**Status**|For example: not started, in progress, under review, or completed|
|**Dependencies**|Any task or decision that must be completed first, where relevant|

Include research, Markdown development, content checking, production, test-bank preparation, mini-lecture preparation, peer review, revision, and documentation.

Tasks should be small enough to assign and check. For example, **“Draft and verify the explanation of the fetch–decode–execute cycle”** is more useful than **“Do hardware.”**
