## GitHub Repository and Markdown Content V1.0 (30XP)

**Opened:** 星期一, 7 九月 2026, 12:25 AM

**Due:** 星期六, 24 十月 2026, 11:54 PM

**1. General Instructions**

Establish your team’s GitHub repository and develop the first substantial version of your module’s learning content in Markdown.

**Markdown is the shared content foundation for your Study Guide, PPT Slides, and Video Tutorials.** Use this stage to investigate the subject, organize the learning sequence, and develop accurate explanations, examples, and activities before full production.

**V1.0 must contain substantive learning content, not merely headings, copied notes, or a plan for future writing.** It does not need to be the finished module, but it must provide enough developed material for the teacher to assess your understanding, coverage, and teaching approach.

Use GitHub throughout the work to coordinate contributions and preserve development history.

**Your submission must be self-contained.** Submit the actual content and readable evidence of repository development. The teacher may assess the assignment without opening your GitHub link.

Read the attached **Shared Assignment Rules**, particularly the requirements for AI-use records, source attribution, individual contributions, and complete submissions.

**2. Specific Requirements**

**Assignment type:** Team assignment — one submission per team  
**Deadline:** **October 24, 2026, at 23:59 (Beijing time)**

**A. Establish and Organize the Repository**

Create one shared GitHub repository for the team project. Ensure that all members can contribute and that the teacher can access it if needed.

Include:

· A **README** identifying the team, selected module, members, and their GitHub usernames.

· A clear explanation of the project’s purpose and intended learners.

· An overview of the folder structure and where to find the content.

· The shared Markdown content and supporting assets, such as images, diagrams, and example files.

· The current project plan and task list, or a clearly identified location for them.

· A brief statement of the project’s current progress and remaining work.

Use meaningful file and folder names. Keep images and other supporting assets organized, and use relative paths where appropriate so that the submitted copy works outside GitHub.

**Do not place personal logs or private information in a publicly accessible repository.**

**B. Develop the Module Structure**

Provide an outline covering the whole selected module, including:

· Intended learning outcomes: what beginners should be able to explain or do.

· Major topics and subtopics in a logical learning sequence.

· Any prerequisite knowledge learners will need.

· Planned examples, demonstrations, questions, or activities.

· The current development status of each section.

Use clear status labels, such as **outline only**, **drafted**, **checked**, or **needs revision**. Identify important gaps and uncertainties honestly.

The outline should show how the content forms a coherent learning resource rather than a collection of unrelated facts.

**C. Write Substantive Markdown Content V1.0**

Begin developing the actual learning material across the module. Fully develop **at least one coherent topic or connected group of subtopics** sufficiently to demonstrate your intended teaching approach.

The developed material must include:

· **Explanations:** Accurate, beginner-friendly explanations of the main concepts.

· **Terminology:** Definitions of unfamiliar terms when they first appear.

· **A worked example or demonstration:** A step-by-step illustration showing how an idea works.

· **A meaningful visual where useful:** A diagram, table, annotated image, or other visual that helps explain the content.

· **A learner activity or application question:** Something that requires learners to use the idea.

· **An answer or expected outcome:** An explanation that allows the learner and teacher to check understanding.

· **References:** Identifiable sources supporting the content and any borrowed material.

**One developed topic is a minimum demonstration of quality, not a substitute for meaningful progress on the rest of the module.** The submission should show both the overall structure and substantive work beyond an empty outline.

Use headings, lists, tables, images, and code blocks appropriately. Prioritize clear content and organization over decorative formatting.

Do not simply paste textbook passages, previous students’ work, or AI output into Markdown. Check the material, organize it for your learners, and be prepared to explain it.

**D. Check the Content and Plan Its Use**

Have another team member review the developed material.

Check:

· Whether definitions, claims, examples, and answers are correct.

· Whether beginners can follow the explanation without unexplained prerequisites.

· Whether activities can be completed using the information provided.

· Whether sources exist and support the relevant claims.

· Whether images, file references, and supporting assets work in the submitted copy.

Record at least one concrete review outcome: what was checked, what was found, and what was corrected or confirmed. You do not need to invent a problem if the reviewed content is correct.

Briefly explain how the developed content could become:

· A section of the **Study Guide**.

· A visual explanation in the **PPT Slides**.

· A focused explanation or demonstration in a **Video Tutorial**.

You are not required to produce all three finished resources for this assignment.

**E. Provide Evidence of GitHub Use and Contributions**

Include a clearly labelled **Repository Evidence** section in your submission report.

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Evidence**|**What it should show**|
|**Repository overview**|Repository name, README, and main files or folders.|
|**Development history**|Readable commit history showing authors, dates, and meaningful descriptions of changes.|
|**Examples of actual changes**|Selected change views showing what was added, corrected, or improved—not just a list of commit messages.|
|**Individual contributions**|Each member’s work, the relevant files or sections, and evidence supporting the claimed contribution.|
|**Review and coordination**|A representative example of content review, task coordination, or resolving a problem. This may come from GitHub or your documented team discussions.|

Use readable screenshots and selected text excerpts, with brief captions explaining what each item demonstrates. Several screenshots may be needed; do not shrink an entire screen until the text becomes unreadable.

Every member must make a substantive contribution. Where practical, members should commit their own changes using identifiable accounts. If one member uploads work on behalf of another, identify both the author and uploader and provide supporting evidence.

**A repository link, a screenshot of the repository homepage, or commit counts alone are insufficient evidence of development or contribution.** Do not create artificial commits merely to increase activity counts.

**All content and evidence required for assessment must be inside the submitted package.** GitHub and other links may provide supplementary access, but they must not replace submitted content. Required material available only through a link will be treated as missing.

Include relevant AI-use records in the project and personal logs according to Section 6 of the Shared Assignment Rules. The actual interaction and verification evidence must be included in the submitted logs, not available only through conversation links.

Keep using and updating the repository after this deadline. This submission is the **Stage 1 snapshot**, not the end of repository development.
